#+TITLE: MVVM-Style Layering Under Compute Pressure — A Pattern-Level Critique
#+DATE: 2026-08-13
#+PANOPTES_ANGLE: 02-mvvm-critique

* Summary

The suspicion "MVVM works great when you have microservices and a CRUD app, and compute land is idiosyncratic" is *partially true and partially mis-specified*. What the credible sources actually support: (1) MVVM's binding machinery is tuned for *user-driven, low-frequency, form-over-data* state — its canonical domain is enterprise LOB apps, and its own inventor and the peer-reviewed literature document the cost ("overkill for simple UI", ViewModel bloat, hard-to-debug declarative bindings, async/thread gaps). (2) The *transferable core* of the whole MVVM/MVC/hexagonal family is not bindings at all — it is separated presentation plus a dependency rule keeping the compute core UI-free and headlessly testable; that core transfers perfectly to compute apps and is exactly what ParaView, VTK, and clean/hexagonal guidance demonstrate. (3) Compute apps are idiosyncratic in the *data path* (pipeline/stream, high-frequency updates, thread affinity), which is where binding-centric MVVM genuinely breaks — the fix is dataflow/stream mechanics (queue, throttle, coalesce), not more layers. Verdict for JTML: keep the pure domain core and the headless coordinator (that part is grounded), keep Presentation-Model-style VMs only for real form state (e.g. SettingsBridge), and treat the GPU-optimizer progress path as a dataflow problem, not a binding problem.

* Key Findings

** Finding 1 — MVVM's inventor documented the applicability limits; "CRUD-tuned" is close but the mechanism mismatch is frequency, not CRUD-ness
- John Gossman (who introduced MVVM at Microsoft for WPF/Silverlight, 2005): "For simple UI, M-V-VM can be overkill. In bigger cases, it can be hard to design the ViewModel up front in order to get the right amount of generality. Data-binding for all its wonders is declarative and harder to debug than nice imperative stuff..." — the pattern's creator, not a detractor [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model%E2%80%93view%E2%80%93viewmodel ; https://stackoverflow.com/questions/883895/what-are-the-problems-of-the-mvvm-pattern]
- Wikipedia on Gossman's stated criticisms: MVVM can be "overkill" for simple UIs; generalizing the ViewModel up front is difficult in larger apps; "large-scale data binding can lead to lower performance" [Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model%E2%80%93view%E2%80%93viewmodel]
- The canonical MVVM literature is uniformly enterprise/LOB: Josh Smith's foundational MSDN article (Feb 2009) demonstrates a WPF app over a data service and pitches unit-testable ViewModels; the 2025 academic multivocal review frames MVVM for "complex GUIs in enterprise software systems" [Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/archive/msdn-magazine/2009/february/patterns-wpf-apps-with-the-model-view-viewmodel-design-pattern ; https://arxiv.org/html/2504.18191v1]
- The peer-reviewed MVVM Revisited MLR (Speth & Becker, U. Stuttgart, 2025; 38 papers + 125 websites) lists as *standard drawbacks*: complexity for simple GUIs, hard up-front ViewModel design, harder debugging of declarative bindings, and increased memory consumption from binding overhead [Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2504.18191v1]
- Community corroboration: "for a very basic CRUD application [MVVM] seems overkill" [Source: https://www.reddit.com/r/UWP/comments/f8v1y5/is_mvvm_the_only_way/]
- Confidence: high
- Relevance to this project: JTML's QML surface is small and form-like (settings, pose IO, stage control). Binding-heavy MVVM is defensible exactly there. It is NOT defensible as the organizing principle for the optimize loop, the GPU cost function, or the worker thread. The "works great for CRUD" half of the belief is supported; the "therefore default" half is not.

** Finding 2 — Documented failure mode: ViewModel bloat and definitional drift
- 12 of the MLR's sources state the ViewModel usually has "too many responsibilities"; the standard definition is vague enough that inexperienced developers pile responsibilities in without modularization — the review found 76 additional design constructs and 29 design aspects that the "standard" definition simply leaves open [Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2504.18191v1]
- Practitioners concur that the pattern is ambiguous: "MVVM has a problem: everyone has a different definition of what it is and how it should be implemented" [Source: https://www.mrlacey.com/2023/12/is-mvvm-still-relevant-or-useful.html]
- A common iOS critique: "Most MVVM implementations are wrong because they misunderstand data flow, overuse ViewModels as dumb data holders, neglect proper separation of concerns, and misuse bindings" [Source: https://medium.com/@dev_16101/why-most-mvvm-implementations-are-wrong-d5f839b9f1eb — practitioner essay, lower weight]
- Confidence: high (arXiv MLR is systematic; the rest corroborates)
- Relevance: JTML's restructure must state *what a ViewModel is allowed to contain* in this codebase (presentation mapping of form state only; never optimization state, never GPU state). Otherwise the shared VM layer for QML will accrete coordinator logic, which is the documented bloat trajectory.

** Finding 3 — Documented failure mode: the synchronization problem is inherent, not incidental
- Fowler, writing the canonical Presentation Model pattern (which MVVM is a data-binding variant of): "Probably the most annoying part of Presentation Model is the synchronization between Presentation Model and view." His own reference example needs a re-entrancy guard (`_isLoadingView`) to prevent update loops during two-way sync — i.e., the pattern's canonical description ships with a sync-loop guard as part of the recipe [Source: https://martinfowler.com/eaaDev/PresentationModel.html]
- Fowler on observer-based sync (the binding mechanism): "you can't tell what is happening by reading the code... Observer behavior is hard to understand and debug because it's implicit behavior" [Source: https://martinfowler.com/eaaDev/uiArchs.html]
- Nick's .NET Travels "Stop Using MVVM" catalogs the same cluster: boilerplate (INPC/backing fields/commands), distributed state with no single source of truth, command explosion, and implicit flow that is painful to debug [Source: https://nicksnettravels.builttoroam.com/stop-using-mvvm/]
- The MLR found a dedicated design construct, "Asynchronous Results Handling by Mediator", exists precisely because async operations and data bindings not firing on the GUI thread are a known MVVM gap — the standard definition "lack[s] guidance for handling asynchronous data bindings not firing on the GUI thread" [Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2504.18191v1]
- Confidence: high
- Relevance: JTML's OptimizeCoordinator runs a persistent worker thread; the cost path does ~15 kernel launches + ~5 synchronous D2H memcpys per evaluation at ~7k evals/s. Any design that pushes per-evaluation progress through INPC-style notifications inherits the sync problem at pathological frequency. The guard-flag/scatter-property machinery from Finding 3 is the wrong tool for a stream.

** Finding 4 — Compute-specific failure mode: thread affinity makes binding-based sync outright unsafe across the worker boundary
- Qt's own documentation: QObject thread affinity is a hard constraint; "Calling delete on a QObject from a thread other than the one that owns the object (or accessing the object in other ways) is unsafe" [Source: https://doc.qt.io/qt-6/threads-qobject.html]
- Qt practitioner guidance: "Directly modifying [GUI] from a background thread can lead to crashes, visual glitches, or undefined behavior" — the safe patterns are queued signal-slot delivery and explicit marshalling, not property writes [Source: https://linuxvox.com/blog/modify-qt-gui-from-background-worker-thread/]
- Qt's own Model/View framework (QAbstractItemModel) is designed for data *display* of structured lists/trees; it is not an MVVM layer and is documented as such — conflating the two is a known point of confusion in the ecosystem ("Why Qt is misusing model/view terminology?") [Source: https://doc.qt.io/qt-6/model-view-programming.html ; https://stackoverflow.com/questions/5543198/why-qt-is-misusing-model-view-terminology]
- Confidence: high (Qt docs authoritative; the cross-thread claim is boilerplate Qt knowledge)
- Relevance: any ViewModel or QObject model that sits on the GUI thread and is written from the optimizer thread is a crash/UB hazard by construction. The existing architecture's instinct — coordinator state machine on a persistent worker thread, UI updated via queued signals — is correct; formalize it as a *dataflow contract* (see Finding 8), not as a binding contract.

** Finding 5 — When the "model" is a GPU pipeline and there is no view, the ViewModel concept evaporates — by definition
- The ViewModel is "the model for the view" (Josh Smith) and the Presentation Model is explicitly "part of the presentation" (Fowler) — a construct whose entire reason to exist is a stateful, bindable view [Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/archive/msdn-magazine/2009/february/patterns-wpf-apps-with-the-model-view-viewmodel-design-pattern ; https://martinfowler.com/eaaDev/PresentationModel.html]
- A headless optimizer (JTML's OptimizeCoordinator: state machine, worker thread, no widgets) has no view, therefore nothing to be "model-for". The pattern that names its situation is Cockburn's Hexagonal Architecture: "Allow an application to equally be driven by users, programs, automated test or batch scripts, and to be developed and tested in isolation from its eventual run-time devices and databases" — the GUI is one driver among many (GUI, CLI/pvpython-style scripting, tests, batch) [Source: https://dev.to/xoubaman/understanding-hexagonal-architecture-3gk (quoting Cockburn)]
- Uncle Bob's Clean Architecture makes the same point from the other side: "The software in this layer [interface adapters] is a set of adapters... It is this layer, for example, that will wholly contain the MVC architecture of a GUI" — MVC/MVVM are *outer-layer* concerns; the core must not know them [Source: https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2012/08/13/the-clean-architecture.html]
- Confidence: high
- Relevance: JTML's jtml_domain (pure, Qt-free, planned Rust-interop surface) and OptimizeCoordinator already sit where the literature says compute cores should sit: inside the hexagon, UI-ignorant. The planned Rust-interop surface is precisely a *driven port* story. Wrapping the coordinator in ViewModels would invert the dependency direction the sources recommend.

** Finding 6 — Layering helps when it follows the dependency rule and testability seams; the evidence is concrete
- Clean Architecture: systems should be "Independent of UI... The UI can change easily, without changing the rest of the system. A Web UI could be replaced with a console UI... without changing the business rules", with the Dependency Rule (source dependencies point inward) as the mechanism [Source: https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2012/08/13/the-clean-architecture.html]
- Fowler's Layering Principles workshop: strongest votes were "low coupling between layers, high cohesion within them" (10/0), "user interface modules should contain no business logic" (10/0), "business layer only uses abstractions of technological services" (14/0), "layers should be testable individually" (12/0), "layers are a logical artifact that does not imply distribution" (11/0); the "there are at least three main layer types: presentation, domain, data source" claim was *voted down* (3/9) — layer count is cargo-cult, dependency direction is not [Source: https://martinfowler.com/bliki/LayeringPrinciples.html]
- The stack's own reference implementation: VTK is a *dataflow* pipeline (data objects + process objects + directed connections), and ParaView's ServerManager exposes the same pipeline state to GUI and pvpython alike — headless CLI and GUI drive the identical core, which is hexagonal-driving in practice [Source: https://book.vtk.org/en/latest/VTKBook/04Chapter4.html ; https://deepwiki.com/Kitware/ParaView/1.1-architecture ; https://www.paraview.org/paraview-docs/v5.12.0/python/paraview.servermanager.html]
- Confidence: high
- Relevance: JTML's current layers were derived from testability seams and gates (R15 headless tests, injected `std::function` cost, CostFunctionManager variants, stage-graph registry). That derivation matches the literature's justification for layering better than any framework-derived layering would — the External References section of the restructure plan can now cite exactly this.

** Finding 7 — Layering hurts when it is indirection without simplification, abstraction theater, or applied to the hot path
- "If an 'abstraction' isn't hiding complexity but is simply adding a layer of indirection, then it's not an abstraction at all" [Source: https://fhur.me/posts/2024/thats-not-an-abstraction]
- Practitioner consensus on over-application: "Clean Architecture is most beneficial for complex projects with larger teams — for small teams or simple projects, it can become overengineering"; "too many interfaces or too many layers without a clear reason" [Source: https://threedots.tech/episode/is-clean-architecture-overengineering/]
- Testability theater: "And your 'clean architecture' is now just a pile of mocks... By the time your test runs, it's fragile spaghetti: Change a method signature in the repository? Test fails" [Source: https://journal.optivem.com/p/hexagonal-architecture-do-not-mock-everything]
- Compute hot paths: Mike Acton's data-oriented design critique (CppCon 2014 keynote) — "The purpose of all programs, and all parts of those programs, is to transform data from one form to another"; responsibility-based OOP decomposition is "antithetical" to that purpose in performance-critical domains because it scatters data and iteration order [Source: https://cppcon.org/third-keynote-2014/ ; https://calpoly-iandunn.github.io/csc476/lectures/09-data-oriented-design]
- Confidence: medium-high (DOD is a documented school with real shipped precedent at Insomniac; the overengineering critiques are practitioner essays)
- Relevance: JTML's R12 ("no premature abstraction") is the correct guard. The GPU cost function (~7k evals/s, kernel-launch-bound) must never be re-expressed through a service-layer of interfaces; inject seams at the edges (as DirectOptimizer already does) and leave the inner loop flat.

** Finding 8 — The viable alternatives, with trade-offs
- *Presentation Model / MVVM with a Humble View* (Fowler; MLR variants): keep the presentation model for genuine form state; view stays dumb; trade-off is the synchronization cost — acceptable at low frequency, wrong at stream frequency [Source: https://martinfowler.com/eaaDev/PresentationModel.html ; https://arxiv.org/html/2504.18191v1]
- *Passive View / Supervising Controller* (Fowler): presentation logic pulled into a controller; less mapping, fewer classes; the view stays thin without binding machinery — a good fit for the small QML surface if bindings start hurting [Source: https://martinfowler.com/eaaDev/uiArchs.html (pattern family covered there)]
- *Ports and Adapters / Hexagonal* (Cockburn): core + driver ports (GUI, CLI, tests, batch) and driven ports (files, GPU device abstraction, Rust interop); explicitly "no recipe for the number of layers" — it protects boundaries, not layer counts [Source: https://dev.to/xoubaman/understanding-hexagonal-architecture-3gk]
- *Clean Architecture dependency rule* (Martin): same core-purity goal, more prescriptive circles; risk of overengineering for small apps [Source: https://blog.cleancoder.com/uncle-bob/2012/08/13/the-clean-architecture.html ; https://threedots.tech/episode/is-clean-architecture-overengineering/]
- *Dataflow-driven* (VTK pipeline; ParaView state manager): the stack's native idiom — the optimizer progress stream is pushed through queued/coalesced updates (timer-throttled rendering, ring buffers of timestamped samples) rather than observed via bindings [Source: https://book.vtk.org/en/latest/VTKBook/04Chapter4.html ; https://deepwiki.com/Kitware/ParaView/1.1-architecture]
- *MVU / unidirectional data flow* (Elm, Redux, Uno's MVUX): single immutable state + pure reducers + render-from-state; directly addresses MVVM's distributed-state and sync weaknesses; trade-off is a paradigm shift and state-copying cost in C++ [Source: https://nicksnettravels.builttoroam.com/stop-using-mvvm/ ; https://platform.uno/articles/mvvm-mvux-state-management/]
- *Mediator for async results* (documented MVVM gap-filler): route async/compute results through a mediator so bindings are never fired from worker threads [Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2504.18191v1]
- *Data-oriented design* (Acton): for the hot loop only; decompose by data layout; explicitly not a UI pattern [Source: https://cppcon.org/third-keynote-2014/]
- Confidence: high for the existence and trade-off framing of each (canonical sources); medium for fit-to-JTML (that is angle 07's call)
- Relevance: the menu for angle 07 is essentially: hexagon/ports framing for the compute core + Presentation-Model-for-form-state + dataflow contract for the progress path + flat inner loop. MVVM-as-full-app-structure is the one option the evidence argues against.

** Finding 9 — Immediate vs retained UI: the high-frequency-sync friction is real but the retained-mode side is not a strawman
- Games use immediate-mode GUI because of game-loop integration and GPU overlay constraints — "The GUI needs to be updated in sync with the game... it's difficult to integrate traditional GUIs event loops into the game loop" — but multiple practitioners in the same thread counter that retained mode with dirty-rect updates is usually *faster*, and that AAA games in practice use custom retained/scene-graph UI [Source: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=19768156]
- In the game context, the MVVM-relevant note: "You may run into performance problems, since MVVM typically leads to a lot of Data Binding... Most likely it'll be the AI that's gating rather than the UI integration" [Source: https://stackoverflow.com/questions/2529697/how-would-mvvm-be-for-games]
- Confidence: medium (thread/forum-level evidence, but multi-party and technically detailed)
- Relevance: JTML is not a game; Qt6 + QML (retained, scene-graph-backed) is the right rendering substrate. The lesson transfers at the pattern level: keep UI update frequency decoupled from compute frequency (render at 30–60 Hz from coalesced state; never one INPC per evaluation).

** Finding 10 — The strongest argument the suspicion is right, and the strongest it is wrong
- Strongest FOR ("compute land is idiosyncratic, generic patterns don't transfer"): MVVM's mechanism is a view-driven, bidirectional, low-frequency contract (bindings + INPC + commands). A GPU optimizer is a data-driven, unidirectional, high-frequency stream. Every documented failure mode above (sync guards, distributed state, async/thread gaps, debug friction, ViewModel bloat) is the *binding layer* failing under exactly this load. The evidence supports: MVVM's machinery is tuned for form-over-data LOB apps, i.e., the user's "CRUD app" is the home turf, and compute is not [Source: https://martinfowler.com/eaaDev/PresentationModel.html ; https://arxiv.org/html/2504.18191v1 ; https://doc.qt.io/qt-6/threads-qobject.html]
- Strongest AGAINST (the "CRUD-tuned" claim overstates it): MVVM is not "CRUD" — it is *separated presentation*, and the separation core (UI-free domain, dependency rule, testable-without-UI) is exactly what compute apps need and what the same stack's flagship tools do. ParaView/VTK are UI-independent cores + dataflow pipelines + state managers, not ViewModel hierarchies. Fowler's Presentation Model, Cockburn's hexagon, and Martin's dependency rule are the *same family* of ideas; a compute app that keeps its core pure and its UI adapters thin is already doing the transferable 80% [Source: https://martinfowler.com/eaaDev/uiArchs.html ; https://book.vtk.org/en/latest/VTKBook/04Chapter4.html ; https://dev.to/xoubaman/understanding-hexagonal-architecture-3gk]
- Also note: "microservices" are server-side; MVVM is client-side. The user's pairing describes a web LOB stack where MVVM genuinely is home turf — but the pairing is incidental, not causal; there is no authoritative source tying MVVM's merits to microservices specifically.
- Verdict: PARTIAL. The user is right that compute apps are idiosyncratic where it matters (data path, threading, sync frequency) and that the generic binding machinery is CRUD/LOB-tuned. The user is wrong if they conclude the whole pattern family doesn't transfer: the separated-presentation core, the dependency rule, and ports-and-adapters framing are the *primary* literature-supported structure for a compute core like JTML's.
- Confidence: high (this verdict is a synthesis of the cited evidence, not a new claim)

* What Could Not Be Verified
- No large, documented compute-heavy desktop application was found that runs strict end-to-end MVVM (ViewModel per view, full binding) successfully in production — the literature and gray material that exists is either enterprise LOB (positive) or game/real-time (negative or mixed). This is absence of evidence; a systematic case-study pass is angle 06's job.
- No quantitative benchmark of binding overhead specifically for Qt6/QML (the "increased memory consumption by binding overhead" and "large-scale data binding → lower performance" claims are WPF-era and qualitative).
- The specific claim that "MVVM works great with microservices" has no authoritative source at all — it appears to be a colloquial pairing (client-side pattern + server-side buzzword), not a documented architectural claim.
- The "15–20% of CPU per frame rebuilding the UI vertex buffer" figure from the immediate-vs-retained discussion is a single HN anecdote, not a benchmark.
- Whether the arXiv MLR's "better application performance" benefit finding (from mobile empirical studies) would hold for a Qt6/QML desktop compute app — the studies were mobile, not desktop-compute.

**Item 6 — Qt6/KDAB statements on binding overhead — RESOLVED (qualitative + one hard structural number; no per-eval cost figure exists)**

- Qt 6.11 official "Performance considerations and suggestions": binding expressions are "re-evaluated if any of the properties it references are changed... binding expressions should be kept as simple as possible"; the canonical example shows one property-update loop re-evaluating a dependent binding "six times (which then results in any other property bindings which rely on the text value... re-evaluated each time, and lays out the text for display each time)" — i.e., cascade re-evaluation is the documented cost model; "Positioning with bindings... is relatively slow"; "Keep the number of bindings in a delegate to a minimum"; memory has "the fixed and unavoidable overhead of the QML engine (implementation data structures, context information, and so on)" plus per-object "non-optimized binding expressions" in the JS heap [Source: https://doc.qt.io/qt-6/qtquick-performance.html]
- KDAB "QML Engine Internals Part 3: Binding Types" (Erik Verbruggen): "there are hundreds if not thousands of bindings in a typical QML application, therefore a binding needs to be lightweight"; per-binding engine structure QV8Bindings::Binding = exactly 64 bytes (with alignment-bit flag packing); v4 bytecode bindings beat v8 for the typical one-line binding ("the v8 engine has quite a bit of overhead when invoking it and when it needs to call out to the QML engine to resolve objects and properties... All of this is too much overhead for the QML usecase"); author benchmark: evaluating a simple binding a few hundred times, v4 engine faster [Source: https://www.kdab.com/qml-engine-internals-part-3-binding-types/]
- JTML-specific consequence: "the v4 engine can not deal with dynamic objects, for example those exported from C++ via setContextProperty(). A binding containing such a dynamic object will be part of QV8Bindings" (the slower path) [Source: https://www.kdab.com/qml-engine-internals-part-3-binding-types/] — and JTML's QML bridges ARE context-property objects (`engine.rootContext()->setContextProperty(...)` for all five bridges) [Source: src/app/experimental/main.cpp:74-85]. Every QML binding through a bridge property therefore runs on the engine's slower binding path by construction — a structural reason to keep bridge-bound properties few and their expressions trivial, and to keep high-frequency data off Q_PROPERTYs entirely.
- Not verified: no authoritative absolute figure ("binding evaluation costs X ns" / "Y bytes per property") exists for Qt6/QML; the evidence is one hard structural number (64 bytes), a relative path comparison (v4 vs v8), and qualitative guidance. Round-1's "no quantitative Qt6/QML binding-overhead benchmark" note is PARTIALLY resolved.
- Confidence: high for the quoted statements (primary sources), medium for the "slower by construction" JTML inference (it follows directly from the KDAB statement, but no benchmark measures the delta in this app).
- Relevance: it sizes the ceiling — JTML's ~36 form properties are far under the "hundreds to thousands" typical load, but the context-property slow-path nuance argues for keeping the VM surface small and never pushing per-eval data through it.
