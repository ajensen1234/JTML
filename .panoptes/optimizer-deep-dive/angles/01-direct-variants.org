#+TITLE: DIRECT Variant Surface — selection × partitioning × refinement control × hidden constraints
#+DATE: 2026-08-12
#+PANOPTES_ANGLE: direct-variants

* Summary

The papers license a large, well-measured variant surface: three selection schemes (IO/IA/GL) × four partitioning schemes (N-DTC/1-DTC/1-DTDV/1-DBDP), plus refinement-control add-ons (epsilon, delta-limit, median/average, globally-biased, rev) and a hidden-constraint surrogate (DIRECT-GLh). For a 6-DOF search at 20k–35k evaluations, the literature says the current repo configuration — original-DIRECT selection with ε=0 and all ties, 1-DTC partition, L2 size — sits in the worst local-refinement regime; the highest-evidence upgrade is the GL (two-step Pareto) selection combined with 1-DTC or 1-DBDP partitioning, optionally with the globally-biased (-gb) phase switch for refinement control, and the GLh φ = f_min + d(x_min,x) surrogate if unrenderable poses produce NaN. The "restrictions hurt cheap functions" finding (Mathematics 2022, 10, 3760) is real and worse when f_min ≈ 0 (the silhouette-cost shape), and -gb is indeed the only restriction that never hurt in that study — it was also the best on expensive functions. Every variant below can be expressed as an Options struct on DirectOptimizer with bit-identical defaults, and scored headlessly on a DIRECTGOLib/GKLS battery under the production cumulative-budget shape.

* Key Findings

** Finding 1: The repo's current configuration is the literature's worst-case local-refinement regime
- The extracted optimizer is original-DIRECT selection (Jarvis convex hull) with ε=0, selecting *all* hull points including equal-slope ties (`slope >= highest_slope`), trisecting one (largest denormalized) side, L2 size measure (norm of the side vector), center sampling. [Source: /home/ajj/repo/uf/JTML/src/domain/direct_optimizer.cpp L93-141, L143-200; /home/ajj/repo/uf/JTML/src/domain/data_structures_6D.cpp L107-128]
- Jones & Martins 2021: "with ε=0 DIRECT spends so many evaluations refining local minima that it ends up far from the optimum after 3000 evaluations" (Shubert); the ε=0 regime allows "excessive local search". The ε|f_min| rule is what protects the original DIRECT from this — the repo has no such rule at all. [Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.07150 (JOG 79:521–566, DOI 10.1007/s10898-020-00952-6), §3.3/Fig. 19]
- The empirical study's "IO" (improved original) scheme restricts ties to one candidate per (size,value) group; the repo selects all ties, which the study and Jones & Martins show increases "global drag" and slows convergence ("Best results are achieved by ignoring ties for potentially optimal rectangles, only trisecting on one side, and using Gablonsky's modified definition of rectangle size" — Figure 18). [Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.14912 §2.1.1; https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.07150 §3.2]
- Confidence: high (code-verified against primary literature).
- Relevance: the 20k trunk budget is exactly the regime where ε=0 over-refinement wastes evaluations; tie-all selection adds drag on top. Both are cheap to fix (hull tie-break + optional ε filter) with bit-identical defaults preserved.

** Finding 2: GL selection beats IO/IA by ~60–72% on n>4 problems — the regime the 6-DOF search lives in
- On 96 DIRECTGOLib v1.1 box-constrained problems: GL-based variants failed 19/384 cases vs IO 63/384 and IA 55/384; GL required ~60% (vs IO) and ~62% (vs IA) fewer function evaluations overall, and ~72% fewer on n>4 problems. [Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.14912 §4.2]
- Budget-regime crossover (operational characteristics, n>4 multimodal): at ≤500 evals IO variants lead; at ~1000–4000 N-DTC-IO is best (~50% solved); at ≥4000 evals the GL variants (1-DTC-GL, 1-DBDP-GL) are the most efficient. [Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.14912 Fig. 7, §4.2]
- Partitioning for n>4: 1-DBDP-based variants best average (≈16% fewer evals than N-DTC-based); 1-DTDV "very inefficient for higher dimensional test problems"; 1-DTDV shines only when most solution coordinates sit on the boundary (ν≥8/10 coordinates: 551 evals vs 6,487 for N-DTC-IO on Levy10; but 141,999 vs 919 when interior). [Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.14912 §4.1, Table 3]
- On 800 GKLS problems (n=2–5, simple+hard classes): 1-DTC-IO best average (8,021) and best median (1,427); 1-DBDP-GL ranks 5th there but is best on simple classes at budgets >400 evals; IO wins on hard GKLS classes. [Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.14912 §5, Table 6]
- Confidence: high (directly from the study's tables; the study is the repo's own evidence base, papers/ 2022 PDF).
- Relevance: 6-DOF = n>4; 20k trunk = well above the 4000-eval crossover → GL selection is the evidence-backed choice; the partition choice is a tradeoff (1-DTC: best GKLS median + minimal change from current code; 1-DBDP: best n>4 average but requires diagonal-bisection rework). 1-DTDV is a trap unless the pose optimum is confirmed near the range boundary.

** Finding 3: The DIRECT-GLh hidden-constraint surrogate is applicable to NaN/unrenderable poses at negligible cost
- GLh assigns infeasible midpoints the surrogate φ = f_min + d(x_min, x) (Euclidean distance in the *normalized unit cube*), normalized via f̄(x)=(f−f_min)/(f_max−f_min) and d̄=d/d_max so objective and distance are commensurate; f̄_min ≡ 0 drops out. Infeasible points never become f_min (min over feasible only). [Source: thesis §5.2, DOI 10.15388/vu.thesis.138 (mif.vu.lt PDF); DIRECTGO code /tmp clone Algorithms/sDirect_GLh.m Selection()]
- Cost is minimal: the distances d(x_min, x) are *already computed* for the GL two-step selection (the local step is Pareto on (size, distance)); per-iteration cost is one O(N) pass to re-tag infeasible values whenever f_min improves. "Such a technique does not require any additional computation." [Source: arXiv:2107.02205 (DIRECTGO, ACM TOMS 48(4), DOI 10.1145/3559755) §2.3; sDirect_GLh.m]
- Performance on hidden-constraint problems (constraint info withheld, 80 problems): DIRECT-GLh failed 18/80 vs DIRECT-NAS 29/80, DIRECT-Barrier 46/80, subDIRECT-Barrier 47–53/80; GLh took ~4.5× fewer iterations and ~33× less time than NAS. [Source: arXiv:2107.02205 §4.2.1, Table 5]
- DirectGO's own description of why alternatives fail: NaN-valued boxes "would not be selected as potential optimal at all"; barrier penalties leave feasible-edge exploration very slow. [Source: arXiv:2107.02205 §2.3]
- The repo currently passes NaN/Inf straight into storage: `EvaluateCostFunction` has no finite-check; a NaN result fails `result < current_optimum_value_` so it never becomes the optimum, but NaN boxes enter the storage matrix and poison hull slopes in `ConvexHull` (NaN slope comparisons). [Source: /home/ajj/repo/uf/JTML/src/domain/direct_optimizer.cpp L203-220, L93-141]
- Confidence: high for the algorithm and cost; medium for *whether the production GPU silhouette cost actually emits NaN* — no NaN handling exists anywhere in the C++ cost path (only Qt UI validation in app/experimental/PoseBridge.cpp L177-184), so the trigger condition is unverified.
- Relevance: if the GPU cost can fail (degenerate poses, empty silhouette), GLh converts silent corruption into a directed search toward feasibility — and the needed distance data is already there once GL selection lands.

** Finding 4: Refinement control — "restrictions hurt cheap functions" is confirmed, and -gb is the only safe one
- Mathematics 2022, 10, 3760 tested six refinement-reduction add-ons on 1-DTC-GL over 287 DIRECTGOLib v1.2 problems: success rates 1-DTC-GL-gb 0.7840 (225/287) > 1-DTC-GL 0.7805 (224/287) > -limit 0.7631 > -min 0.7526 > -rev 0.7317 > -median 0.7073 > -average 0.6551. Every restriction except gb hurt the base algorithm; gb is "the only algorithm with the same or better performance than the original 1-DTC-GL". [Source: https://epublications.vu.lt/object/elaba:143490244/143490244.pdf Table 2, §3, §4]
- "In the case of cheap test functions, the original 1-DTC-GL is the best of all the algorithms tested, meaning that the local refinement reduction schemes are redundant. However, when the objective functions are expensive, the local refinement reduction techniques improve the performance, and 1-DTC-GL-gb is the best technique among all tested." [Source: same, §3–§4]
- The restriction harm is worst when f_min = 0: "the restriction on selecting small hyper-rectangles may prevent the algorithms from converging to a solution... especially apparent when the solution is equal to zero" (1-DTC-GL-average worsened average evals by 32% overall; suffered most on f*=0 problems). [Source: same, §3]
- gb mechanics (from the DIRECTGO implementation of the studied algorithm): run the traditional two-step selection; after 5 consecutive iterations without f_min improvement, drop the local (distance-based) selection phase, except every 5th iteration ("security" iteration) — `(fMinNotImpr > 5) && (mod(fMinNotImpr,5) ~= 0)` → local phase else global-only. Defaults `ovexpl='GL'`, `locbalance='GL'`, `ep=1e-4`. [Source: /tmp clone of https://github.com/blockchain-group/DIRECTGO, Algorithms/I_DTC_GL.m L122-138, L235-237; GENDIRECT parameter table in README]
- Jones & Martins: the ε=1e-4 default protects the original DIRECT from ε=0's excessive local search; adaptive-ε (Finkel-Kelley DIRECT-restart) is mixed (better on Shubert, worse on Powell-48). [Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.07150 §2.3.2, §3.3]
- Confidence: high (direct quotes from the study that owns this exact question).
- Relevance: JTML's silhouette cost has f_min ≈ 0 and a GPU eval that is expensive (dilation 6/3/1 renders) — the two conditions where restrictions fail and where gb pays. ε-based restrictions (min/median/average) are doubly contraindicated (window ε|f_min| collapses at f_min→0 — the Sergeyev–Kvasov zero-minimum weakness Jones & Martins reproduce). Answer to Q3: yes, -gb is the only refinement control that survives; for cheap analytic battery functions keep it OFF and test the crossover.

* Variant Surface (12 + refinement add-ons)

** Selection schemes
- IO — Improved Original: lower-right convex hull of (size, f(center)) with ε in eq. (4), ties restricted to one candidate per (size,value) group. Current repo = this *without* ε and *with* all ties.
- IA — Improved Aggressive: select the min-value box of every distinct size group, with δ_limit floor (in the study: box subdivided 50n times). Parallel-friendly; worst sequential record in the 2022 study.
- GL — two-step Pareto: step 1 Pareto-nondominated on (size ↑, f(center) ↓); step 2 Pareto-nondominated on (size ↑, Euclidean distance from x_min ↓); union. No ε, no parameters; "does not have any protection against over-exploration in sub-optimal local minima regions".

** Partitioning schemes
- N-DTC — trisect *all* longest sides at once (2k+1 new evals per POH, k = # of max sides); original DIRECT (Jones 1993).
- 1-DTC — trisect the single longest side only (2 evals per POH); tie-break in the study: least-split-so-far dimension, then lowest index. Repo is 1-DTC-alike but ties break by *denormalized* side length (GetLargestDirection on denormalized sides), lowest index on equality — for anisotropic ranges (branch 15,15,25,25,25,25; leaf 3,3,15,3,3,3) the two rules diverge.
- 1-DTDV — trisect single longest side, sample two *diagonal vertices*; boundary-optimum weapon; catastrophic on interior optima / high n.
- 1-DBDP — bisect along the diagonal, sample two interior diagonal points (reusable across descendants); best n>4 average; needs its own storage/diameter bookkeeping.

** Refinement-control add-ons (on top of GL, per Mathematics 2022)
- -min / -median / -average: ε-style floors f_min−ε|f_min|, f_min−ε|f_min−f_median|, f_min−ε|f_min−f_average| — all hurt, worst at f*=0.
- -limit: δ_limit floor (20n subdivisions in the 2022 paper) + always select the f_min box — hurt slightly (0.7805→0.7631 success).
- -gb: globally-biased two-phase switch (drop local phase after 5 non-improving iterations, security iteration every 5th) — the only non-hurting restriction; best on expensive functions.
- -rev: local phase only on the iteration f_min improves — mixed (best median, 5th success rate).
- ε=0 default in the repo vs ε=1e-4 in DIRECTGO defaults: keep 0 (current behavior) for the analytic battery baseline; the ε>0 option exists for the original-selection regime.

* Analytic battery spec (hand-off to angle 03; this angle fixes the surface + scoring)

- Functions (DIRECTGOLib v1.1 set, per Appendix A of arXiv:2109.14912):
  - n=6: Hartman6 (f*=−3.3223) — the only exact-6D multimodal in the library.
  - n=5 (closest 6-DOF stand-in): Ackley, Alpine, Griewank, Rastrigin, Rosenbrock, Schwefel, Levy, Michalewicz, Styblinski-Tang, Deb01, Deb02 (boundary-vertex optimum), Sphere (unimodal control).
  - n=2 cheap multimodal: Goldstein-Price, Branin, Six-Hump-Camel — cheap-function regime where restrictions hurt and IO wins (validate the crossover).
  - f*=0 subset (Ackley/Alpine/Griewank/Rastrigin/Rosenbrock/Schwefel/Levy/Sphere): mirrors the silhouette f_min≈0 shape — must be reported separately, since the papers show restrictions hurt most here.
  - GKLS: 8 classes × 100 instances (n=2,3,4,5; simple + hard) with distance-to-optimum stopping (eq. 10, Δ per class).
- Budgets/termination (production shape):
  - Flat: 20000 (trunk-only) and cumulative 20000/25000/30000/35000 via SetCallOffset to reproduce the 3-stage cascade headlessly.
  - Crossover probe: 100, 500, 1000, 4000 to confirm the IO→GL regime switch at the exact shapes (papers: IO ≤500, N-DTC-IO ~50% @ 1000–4000, GL ≥4000).
  - Scoring: percent-error stop pe ≤ 1e-2 (f*≠0) / pe=f (f*=0), plus operational-characteristics (proportion solved vs budget) and median-evals-to-solve — same metrics as the papers, so results are directly comparable.
  - Determinism: fixed seed-free (DIRECT is deterministic); assert bit-identical baseline reproductions with default Options (R13).
- Hidden-constraint battery (only if GLh lands): analytic functions with an unknown-to-optimizer constraint g(x)≤0 cutting a fraction of the domain (DIRECTGO's 80-problem protocol, arXiv:2107.02205 §4.2.1); score feasibility-discovery rate + solved fraction; verify the NaN path returns sentinel (10^9 in DIRECTGO) and never becomes f_min.

* Recommended default variant for the production budget shape

- Landing variant: **1-DTC-GL-gb** — 1-DTC partition (already the repo's de-facto split rule), GL two-step Pareto selection (n>4, ≥4000-eval regime: ~60–72% fewer evals than IO/IA), locbalance=gb (only refinement control that never hurt; best when evals are expensive — GPU silhouette with dilation 6/3/1). Keep size measure L2 and ε=0 (bit-identical default; no ε-style restrictions — f_min≈0 makes them harmful).
- Stretch variant: 1-DBDP-GL (best n>4 average; needs diagonal-bisection rework of storage/sampling).
- Do not enable 1-DTDV unless the battery (shifted-domain + boundary probes) shows the pose optimum near the range edge; evidence shows it is catastrophic for interior optima.
- Enable the GLh φ-surrogate only if the GPU cost can emit NaN/Inf — this angle could not verify the trigger; that check belongs to angle 02's cost-path audit. If NaN is possible, GLh turns it from silent storage corruption (hull slope NaN) into a feasibility-directed search at near-zero extra cost.
- Ordering per the measurement-first rule: land Options struct with bit-identical defaults first (Tier-1 + Tier-2 gates must stay green on the baseline config), then score 1-DTC-GL vs 1-DTC-GL-gb vs 1-DBDP-GL vs baseline on the battery above; no variant touches production until the harness says so.

** R2-4: GLh integration — the surrogate is f_min + d_unit(x_min,x) with d̄ = d/√n; today a NaN silently corrupts ConvexHull (skip-or-burn); DenormalizeFromCenter must not feed the distance

** R2-6: Contradiction 2, head-on — the variant choice and the z-sensitivity ranking are orthogonal in mechanism; only two second-order interactions exist
- The z-sensitivity ranking (chamfer-heavy ≥ Mahfouz ≥ pure overlap) is a property of the COST FUNCTION at a fixed pose — how much the per-pose score changes under Δz (observability, measured by angle 03's z-profile valley depth). The DIRECT variant (selection × partitioning × refinement control) is a property of the SEARCH SCHEME — which poses get sampled and in what order. A variant cannot change what a fixed cost observes, and no selection scheme can "find" a z that the cost does not see. [Source: angles/03-measurement-apparatus.org z-profile probe; synthesis.org Contradiction 2]
- Documented interactions (both second-order, neither changes the orthogonality of the mechanisms): (i) the NaN/Inf trigger — if the cost fails on some poses, the variant's NaN handling (raw corruption today vs GLh's distance-directed surrogate, R2-4) changes which poses are effectively searched; GLh turns an invisible/ignored infeasible region into a feasibility-directed search, which changes the sampled-pose distribution in exactly the hidden-constraint case the ranking assumes is observable; (ii) refinement control — with a z-flat cost, local refinement around the in-plane optimum cannot improve z, so gb/rev's suppression of the local phase during stagnation is a pure-efficiency win (evals saved, no z change); conversely GL's unconditional local phase wastes evals on z-flat refinement.
