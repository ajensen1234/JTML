* Summary
A cost landscape that is symmetric about an axis has structurally multiple equal-cost global minima (a discrete orbit for mirror symmetry, a whole minimum manifold for continuous rotational symmetry) — no cost fix can remove that ambiguity, because even a perfect cost for a perfectly symmetric implant renders symmetric poses identically. The DIRECT literature documents exactly how a single-path DIRECT reacts: on problems with many equal-(size, value) rectangles the POH set explodes and budget spreads across the symmetric orbit (Stripinis & Paulavičius 2022 empirical study; Gablonsky 2001 thesis, Lemma 4.4), and with a *unique* minimizer DIRECT clusters near it — the contrapositive being that non-unique (symmetric) minima do not get a single cluster. Search-side mitigation is therefore viable and evidence-backed: (1) tie restriction (IO / Gablonsky one-per-group) stops the symmetric POH explosion; (2) GL two-step Pareto selection + gb phase (plan 010's 1-DTC-GL-gb) keeps global exploration of both orbit members alive with documented robustness on multimodal/periodic problems; (3) hypothesis retention from DIRECT's own sampled corpus is *free* (0 GPU evals, DIRECT already stores every point) and is the missing "keep all symmetric hypotheses alive" mechanism — the search hands N hypotheses to angle 02; (4) for high-confidence symmetry, SymDIRECT's fundamental-domain restriction (search one mirror cell, reconstruct the orbit analytically) costs *fewer* evals, at the price of a validated symmetry assumption. Current JTML config (tie=All, ε=0) sits in the literature's worst regime for exactly this failure mode; every fix lands on DirectOptimizer::Options with bit-identical defaults (R15).

- DEFINES the plan-010 vocabulary: DTC = "Dimensional Trisection at Center" partitioning — N-DTC = n-dimensional trisection (original DIRECT), 1-DTC = trisection of the single longest side (JTML's OneSide rule); selection schemes IO (Improved Original), IA (Improved Aggressive), GL (two-step Pareto, ≡ DIRECT-GL); 12 variants = 3×4 cross-product.
- SYMMETRY QUOTE (verbatim): "such a selection strategy can be especially inefficient, e.g., for symmetric problems. To overcome this, authors in [Gablonsky 2001] proposed selecting only one of these many 'equivalent' candidates. In [Jones 2021], the authors revealed that such modification could significantly increase the performance of the DIRECT algorithm." (URL: https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2109.14912)
- Table 4 (evals to solve, DIRECTGOLib): Rastrigin 5D: N-DTC-IO 597, 1-DTC-IO 453, 1-DTC-GL 1,895, N-DTC-GL 2,721; Rastrigin 10D: 1-DTC-IA 7,215, 1-DTC-GL 8,105, N-DTC-GL 22,971, 1-DBDP-GL >1e6.

** E2. Gablonsky 2001 (PhD thesis, NCSU) + Gablonsky & Kelley 2001 DIRECT-l paper — VERIFIED-full-text (thesis extraction in /tmp, prior-run corpus) + abstract.
- Theorem 4.1 + consequence (verbatim): "This theorem also tells us that if we have a unique minimizer, DIRECT's subdivisions become refined near the global minimum. DIRECT clusters the sampling points around the global minimum." Contrapositive for symmetric basins: with 2+ equal minima, no unique cluster — refinement and budget split across the orbit.
- Lemma 4.3 (constant function, DIRECT-l): "If f is a constant function, DIRECT-l divides exactly one hyperrectangle in every iteration" — degenerate slow branching (worst-case p_c^N(k) iterations).
- Lemma 4.4 (constant function, original DIRECT): "If f is constant ... DIRECT divides in every iteration all hyperrectangles with maximum measure, and no others" — POH explosion on flat/equal-value regions; every largest box is potentially optimal every iteration (budget spread). Exact plateau mechanism, quantified.
- DIRECT-l design: "DIRECT was modified specifically for small-dimensional problems with few global minima"; one potentially-optimal hyperrectangle per σ∞ group (the tie-restriction lineage of IO).

* Findings
** F1. A symmetric cost makes the ambiguity structural; DIRECT's documented reaction is budget spread over the equal-cost orbit — search-side mitigation is required, not optional.
- The empirical study names symmetric problems as the POH-explosion case (many equal-(diameter, value) boxes selected per iteration) [E1]; Gablonsky's Lemma 4.4 proves the mechanism on flat functions (all max-measure boxes POH every iteration) [E2]; Theorem 4.1's contrapositive: with non-unique minima there is no single cluster, so refinement splits across the orbit [E2]. For JTML: rotation about the implant symmetry axis makes every image term invariant (local recon) [E15] — the orbit is a minimum manifold, not two isolated points.
- Confidence: high (two primary texts + local recon).
- Relevance: challenges belief (1) head-on: no cost fix removes symmetric minima for a symmetric object; the search must be symmetric-aware (fundamental domain / retention) or disambiguation post-hoc (angle 02) — and per the angle boundary, search keeps hypotheses alive; angle 02 resolves them.
** F2. Tie restriction (IO / one-per-group) is the literature's documented fix for symmetric POH explosion — and JTML currently selects ALL ties.
- Verbatim study quote (E1) + Jones & Martins "best results achieved by ignoring ties" (E3) + Gablonsky's one-POH-per-group DIRECT-l (E2, E14). Current Options.ties = All, ε = 0 (direct_optimizer.h L64-71) is the worst regime (E14). Cost: 0 extra evals; typically fewer evals per iteration on symmetric/flat regions.
- Confidence: high.
- Relevance: un-stub `ties` (add One) FIRST — it is the cheapest, most directly symmetry-targeted fix.
** F3. GL two-step Pareto selection + gb phase (plan 010's 1-DTC-GL-gb) keep both orbit members explored and buy robustness on periodic/multimodal landscapes — but selection variants alone do NOT retain multiple minima.
- GL's step-2 global selection and gb's global-only stagnation phase keep large boxes (other orbit cells) in play [E1, E7, E14]; empirical: 1-DTC-GL 24,119 evals on perturbed Rastrigin 5D vs 1-DTC-IO 567,269 — GL is the insurance against periodicity misalignment [E1]. GLce-type selection bounds per-iteration POH growth [E8]; GLce-min is the best DIRECT-type at 10^3-10^4 budgets [E9]. GL wins ~60-72% fewer evals than IO/IA on n>4 [E14].
- Caveat: GL alone returns ONE point (GetOptimumLocation) — the symmetric twin is silently dropped. Retention (F7) is the missing piece.
- Confidence: high (verified table numbers + primary-text gb prose).
- Relevance: plan 010's direction is correct but incomplete for the symmetry case — add retention/output.
** F4. 1-DTC partitioning is the right base for multimodal periodic landscapes but is fragile under periodicity misalignment — the rotation-DOF wrap risk.
- 1-DTC dominates Rastrigin/Griewank originals; perturbed-domain collapse (E1). For JTML rotations (360° wrap), the true pose's periodic replicate can sit off the center-sampling lattice; bounds perturbation (equivalent to phase shift) is exactly the documented failure case. Mitigations: GL selection (F3), ε floor (small positive ε per Jones 1993 calibration [E3, E14] — note the ε|f_min| filter is inert at f_min=0), and fundamental-domain restriction (F5).
- Confidence: high (mechanism), medium (transfer to pose-DOF periodicity — inference).
** F5. Fundamental-domain search (SymDIRECT pattern) is the canonical symmetry-aware DIRECT: search one orbit cell, reconstruct the rest analytically — zero extra evals, fewer evals total; requires a VERIFIED symmetry.
- SymDIRECT restricts to one hypertetrahedron with linear constraints [E5]; DIRECTGO ships it as glbSolve-sym/sym2 [E6]. For JTML the cheap form: a half-range bound on one Euler angle (mirror cell) or a 1/N sector (rotational symmetry), with the mirror transform supplied by the existing dyad geometry (compute_mirror_pose) [E15]. Cost: negative (≈half the domain). Risk: breaks if the implant is not exactly symmetric — must be gated on verified geometry (angle 01/05) and harness-arbitrated (R9), never default.
- Confidence: medium-high (method VERIFIED; JTML applicability INFERRED).
** F6. Hypothesis retention from DIRECT's own sampled corpus is FREE and is the core "keep symmetric hypotheses alive" mechanism: N hypotheses out, handoff to angle 02.
- DIRECT never forgets sampled points (E12); DirectDataStorage already stores every eval. Cluster the stored low-cost points (glcCluster-style, E9/TOMLAB; or DIRECTGO's GENDIRECT Clustering.m component, E6) into k distinct basins → return top-k {Point6D, cost} via a new GetHypotheses() accessor. 0 GPU evals; CPU-only. This is the search-side deliverable angle 02 consumes ("search returned N hypotheses").
- Confidence: high (mechanism verified; clustering quality on the render-cost corpus is the open measurement).

** F8. Inline symmetry detection is cheap two ways; both fit the 20k-35k budget.
- (a) Behavioral fingerprint at 0 evals: persistent high tie-count of equal-(diameter, value) POHs in the ConvexHull output is the documented signature of symmetric/flat structure [E1, E2] — the hull code already computes it; just count. (b) Explicit orbit check at 1 eval each: with the mirror transform known a priori from implant geometry [E15], evaluate f(M(x)) for a sample subset; |f(x)−f(M(x))| ≈ 0 within render noise ⇒ symmetry confirmed (300 checks ≈ 43 ms, 0.9% budget [E16]). Both feed a `symmetry_detected` flag that the harness (not the default path) may act on.
- Confidence: high (mechanism), medium (render-noise tolerance is a measurement for angle 01).
** F9. Staying DIRECT-family is defensible at 6-DOF: deterministic solvers own the low-budget regime.
- 16/16 Q1 solvers deterministic at low budget; DIRECT-type excellent on GKLS 2-6D; stochastic only wins ≥10D [E4]; GLce-min best at 10^3-10^4 [E9]; DIRECT viable ≤5-6D [E12, E13]. No reason to swap search family; reason to add symmetry-specific mechanisms to the existing seam.
- Confidence: high.

* Implications for JTML (concrete)
** Verdict on the three user beliefs
- Belief 1 (blame the cost): REJECTED as the whole story. The ambiguity is structural (F1): a symmetric object has symmetric minima for ANY perfect cost. The cost can only be *helped* (angle 01) or the search made symmetric-aware (this angle) or disambiguation done post-hoc (angle 02). The repo's own recon proves the invariant rotation axis exists in every current image term [E15].
- Belief 2 (DIRECT fine, fix the cost): REJECTED. DIRECT's documented symmetric/flat behavior (POH explosion, budget spread, single-basin clustering) [E1, E2, E10] plus JTML's worst-regime config (all ties, ε=0) [E14] is the exact combination the literature says wastes budget on symmetric problems.
- Belief 3 (plan 010 1-DTC-GL-gb addresses the symmetric case): PARTIALLY ACCEPTED. 1-DTC-GL-gb improves robustness and keeps orbit exploration alive (F3) but returns one point; the missing piece is retention + output of N hypotheses (F6) and, for verified symmetry, the fundamental-domain option (F5). These are additive Options fields with bit-identical defaults.
