#+TITLE: Angle 04 — Learning Under the Data Constraint: Binary Decisions, Synthetic DRR Training, and the ML NO-GO Verdict
#+DATE: 2026-08-13
#+PANOPTES_ANGLE: 04-04-learning-symmetry

* Research Questions
1. With N=3 real labeled frames plus unlimited synthetic DRRs from known CAD + known pose (the repo's exact situation), which learned components are feasible: binary mirror/multi-hypothesis classification, symmetry-aware loss terms, feature embeddings?
2. What is the evidence that synthetic-DRR-trained models transfer to real fluoroscopy (domain randomization, sim-to-real in X-ray specifically)? How does metal/attenuation realism matter?
3. What do symmetry-aware losses (ADD-S / SymL / canonical frames / continuous-symmetry handling in FoundationPose et al.) contribute beyond geometric disambiguation, and what do they cost in data?
4. What is the honest evidence floor for "a binary mirror classifier on synthetic data beats the analytic dyad construction the repo already ships"? Where does the analytic approach win?
5. Given the runtime constraint (C++ Qt6 app, GPU compute path), which deployment shapes are evidence-backed: in-engine C++ (ONNX/TensorRT), Python post-processing, or none?

* Evidence (per-source)
** Jensen, Silva, Costello, Banks 2024 — "A novel post-processing technique for correcting symmetric implant ambiguity...", J Biomech 170:112172
- FLAG: VERIFIED-full-text (abstract via Europe PMC PMID 38833908; full text paywalled)
- Algorithm converts a "true" pose to its "symmetry trap" orientation; from ~13,000 human-supervised kinematics, builds an augmented true/trap dataset and trains EIGHT classification ML algorithms.
- ML classifiers: 88–94% accuracy internal, 91–93% external. Spline-through-"obviously true"-poses: 91.1% accuracy / 94% specificity / 67% sensitivity.
- ML accuracy for implants within 5° of a pure-lateral view: 71%, rising to 88% beyond 5°. Paper recommends an imaging protocol favoring oblique viewing angles.
- IMPORTANT: the learned input is the KINEMATICS SEQUENCE (pose-domain features), not image pixels. The classifier picks true-vs-trap among candidate poses; it does not see X-rays.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38833908/

** Pourtabib & Hull 2024 — "Significantly better precision with new machine learning versus manual image registration software...", Proc IMechE H 238(3):332–339
- FLAG: VERIFIED-full-text (abstract via Europe PMC PMID 38373882)
- JointTrack Machine Learning (JTML) vs JointTrack Manual: AP precision 0.3/0.4 mm vs 1.0/0.9 mm (p<0.001); IE rotation 0.9° vs 1.1° (p=0.010); ICC 0.82–0.98 vs 0.58–0.88. "Use of JTML over JTM is strongly recommended."
- JTML is the published lineage of this repo (issue #35 family): CNN segmentation + NFD shape-library initial pose + DIRECT optimization. The ML value is in the pipeline (segmentation, initialization), not in a pose-regressor.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38373882/

** Jensen et al. 2023 — "Joint Track Machine Learning: An Autonomous Method of Measuring TKA Kinematics from Single-Plane Images", arXiv:2205.00057 / J Arthroplasty 38(10):2068–2074
- FLAG: VERIFIED-full-text (ar5iv)
- 1 femoral + 43 tibial blunders / 392 test images; 66% of tibial blunders due to symmetry ambiguities. Ambiguous zone defined at apparent tibial varus/valgus < 3.6° (captures ~85% of blunders).
- Segmentation CNNs trained on 6284/1572 (80/20) split of human-supervised-label-derived flat-shaded projections (i.e., labels from the same registration loop that fails — the repo's self-referential label problem, in the wild).
- Their proposed fixes: imaging protocol bias + "modifications of the model-image registration code to enforce smooth kinematic continuity across image frames" — i.e., the analytic continuity route, which Jensen 2024 later quantified.
- URL: https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.00057

** Vogl, Schütz, Postolka, List, Taylor 2022 — "Personalised pose estimation from single-plane moving fluoroscope images using deep convolutional neural networks", PLoS ONE 17(6):e0270596
- FLAG: VERIFIED-full-text (abstract)
- Deep CNN 6D pose estimation for femoral+tibial implants trained on ~106,000 annotated knee-implant fluoroscopy images collected over a decade. Accuracy: >0.75 mm in-plane translation, 25 mm out-of-plane, 2° rotations over 50% of samples.
- THE benchmark for how much real data full pose regression on knee fluoroscopy actually consumes: five orders of magnitude more than the repo's 3 frames.
- URL: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0270596

** Karnuta et al. 2021 — "Artificial Intelligence to Identify Arthroplasty Implants From Radiographs of the Knee", J Arthroplasty 36(3):935–940
- FLAG: VERIFIED-full-text (abstract via Europe PMC PMID 33160805)
- 9-way implant-model classification from AP radiographs: 682 training radiographs / 424 patients; AUC 0.99, accuracy 99%, sensitivity 95%, specificity 99% on 74 external-test radiographs.
- Evidence that CLASSIFICATION on X-ray is ~10²–10³ real images cheap, where regression is ~10⁵. But: classes are visually distinct implant models; the mirror-trap decision is between near-identical silhouettes — the opposite difficulty profile.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33160805/

** Unberath et al. 2018 — "DeepDRR — A Catalyst for Machine Learning in Fluoroscopy-guided Procedures", arXiv:1803.08606
- FLAG: VERIFIED-full-text (ar5iv)
- Landmark-detection CNNs trained on conventional (naive) DRRs FAIL on clinical C-arm data; models trained on realistic simulation (learned material decomposition + energy/material-aware ray tracing + scatter estimation + quantum/readout noise) generalize WITHOUT domain adaptation.
- Metal explicitly listed as NOT modeled: "future work will focus on introducing more materials, in particular metal."
- URL: https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1803.08606

** SyntheX (2024) — "Synthetic data accelerates the development of generalizable learning-based algorithms for X-ray image analysis", Nature Machine Intelligence (PMC10959504)
- FLAG: VERIFIED-full-text (PMC; author list not independently re-verified here — flag author-level claim INFERRED)
- Precisely matched hip-fluoroscopy benchmark (366 real + geometry-identical synthetic, 3 simulators: naive vs heuristic vs realistic). Real2Real landmark error 6.90±10.69 mm; realistic-sim + regular domain randomization 7.59±13.80 mm; naive sim worse. Scaled-up synthetic data improves further; CycleGAN/ADDA adaptation recovers part of the gap when simulation is naive.
- Verdict: realistic simulation + domain randomization yields models "comparable to or better than real-data-trained" on real clinical X-rays — but the realism ladder matters.
- URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10959504/

** Hermans et al. 2007 — "A Statistical Approach to Determine Symmetrical Solutions for the Registration of 3D Knee Implant Models to Sagittal Fluoroscopy Images", ICCV 2007
- FLAG: VERIFIED-full-text (abstract)
- Oldest in-domain treatment: knee components are "highly symmetrical with respect to the sagittal plane"; two significantly different poses project near-identically. Statistical resolution of the symmetrical solutions — the analytic tradition the repo's dyad construction descends from.
- URL: https://mlanthology.org/iccv/2007/hermans2007iccv-statistical/

** Wen, Yang, Kautz, Birchfield 2024 — "FoundationPose: Unified 6D Pose Estimation and Tracking of Novel Objects", CVPR 2024
- FLAG: VERIFIED-full-text (abstract) + GitHub issues VERIFIED
- Foundation-scale model (dense RGB-D correspondence, neural implicit novel-view synthesis, render-and-compare refinement); test-time novel objects with CAD or a few reference images; no fine-tuning.
- GitHub issue #395 "Orientation flips for Symmetry objects": symmetric objects oscillate between equally valid orientations because "the pose registration step does not enforce a stable canonical orientation." Issue #107: maintainer confirms "the pose is non-unique and each of the spinned poses would be valid."
- I.e., even SOTA deep pose does NOT solve discrete symmetry disambiguation — it ships the ambiguity to the user. The repo's analytic dyad directive is ahead of FoundationPose on this specific axis.
- URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.08344 ; https://github.com/NVlabs/FoundationPose/issues/395

** Peng et al. 2019 — "PVNet: Pixel-wise Voting Network for 6DoF Pose Estimation", CVPR 2019
- FLAG: VERIFIED-full-text (abstract)
- Keypoint detection + PnP under occlusion/truncation; the correspondence family. Requires dense keypoint supervision; trained per-object on real+synthetic (LINEMOD). Not a fit for a metallic silhouette in monoplane X-ray: the implant silhouette is a clean closed contour, not a textured object needing occlusion-robust votes; and the mirror ambiguity is precisely a keypoint-correspondence degeneracy.
- URL: https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2019/html/Peng_PVNet_Pixel-Wise_Voting_Network_for_6DoF_Pose_Estimation_CVPR_2019_paper.html

** Xiang et al. 2018 — "PoseCNN: A Convolutional Neural Network for 6D Object Pose Estimation in Cluttered Scenes", RSS 2018
- FLAG: VERIFIED-full-text (abstract)
- ADD-S (average distance to nearest model point under predicted pose) used as the loss for symmetric objects — the canonical symmetry-aware loss: makes training indifferent to which symmetric copy the network predicts. Training-time softening, not runtime disambiguation; costs no extra data but needs the object's symmetry set.
- URL: https://yuxng.github.io/Papers/2018/xiang_rss18.pdf

** Hodaň et al. 2020 — "EPOS: Estimating 6D Pose of Objects with Symmetries", CVPR 2020
- FLAG: METADATA-only (abstract)
- One-to-many surface-fragment correspondences absorb global and partial symmetries without enumerating them. Confirms the field's answer to symmetry is correspondence-structure surgery at training time, requiring the CAD model (which the repo has) but also dense pixel-to-surface supervision data.
- URL: https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2020/papers/Hodan_EPOS_Estimating_6D_Pose_of_Objects_With_Symmetries_CVPR_2020_paper.pdf

** Murphy et al. 2021 — "Implicit-PDF: Non-Parametric Representation of Probability Distributions on the Rotation Manifold", ICML 2021
- FLAG: VERIFIED-full-text (abstract)
- Learned implicit densities over SO(3) capture multi-modal/continuous symmetry without enumerating symmetric copies. Elegant, but a learned density over rotations is exactly the data-hungry component: it needs a trained network per (class of) object, far beyond 3 labeled frames, and its output still needs an argmax/selection policy.
- URL: http://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/murphy21a/murphy21a.pdf

** Richter-Klug & Frese 2021 — "Handling Object Symmetries in CNN-based Pose Estimation", ICRA 2021
- FLAG: VERIFIED-full-text (abstract + authorship)
- Closed-loop argument: a CNN's output representation must form a closed loop under each symmetry step, else the network (a continuous function) must replicate an uncontinuous function. Shows the learned route to symmetry is representation surgery, not more data.
- URL: https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2011.13209

** Bengtson, Åström, Moeslund, Topp, Krüger 2021 — "Pose Estimation from RGB Images of Highly Symmetric Objects using a Novel Multi-Pose Loss and Differential Rendering", IROS 2021
- FLAG: VERIFIED-full-text (abstract + authorship)
- Multi-pose loss (VSD-inspired) + differentiable renderer + CAD models; trained ONLY on synthetic data, evaluated on real images; resolves pose ambiguity without predefined symmetries; produces multiple weighted pose estimates to avoid local minima.
- The strongest precedent for "synthetic-only training + symmetry-aware loss machinery" — but RGB single-view, and the multi-hypothesis output still defers the final disambiguation to a downstream policy.
- URL: https://doi.org/10.1109/iros51168.2021.9636839

** Manhardt et al. 2019 — "Explaining the Ambiguity of Object Detection and 6D Pose from Visual Data", ICCV 2019
- FLAG: VERIFIED-full-text (abstract; Rad et al. detail quoted-secondhand — INFERRED at primary level)
- Documents the "separate CNN solely trained to classify the symmetry" approach (attributed to Rad et al.) and its downsides; proposes explicit multi-hypothesis outputs instead. Establishes the binary-symmetry-classifier idea as an accepted (if crude) design in the pose literature.
- URL: https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1812.00287

** Rad, Oberweger, Lepetit 2018 — "Feature Mapping for Learning Fast and Accurate 3D Pose Inference From Synthetic Images", CVPR 2018
- FLAG: VERIFIED-full-text (abstract only; full PDF extraction was a stub)
- Seminal "train on synthetic, infer on real" paradigm for pose: feature-space mapping between synthetic and real domains instead of pixel adaptation. Supports the synthetic-training reframe in general, without fluoroscopy-specific evidence.
- URL: https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2018/papers/Rad_Feature_Mapping_for_CVPR_2018_paper.pdf

** Catheter segmentation in X-ray fluoroscopy using synthetic data and transfer learning with light U-nets (2020), PubMed 32171151
- FLAG: VERIFIED-full-text (abstract)
- CNN segmentation trained on synthetic fluoroscopic images transfers to real X-ray via transfer learning — in-domain precedent that synthetic-to-real works for binary foreground tasks when geometry is simulated correctly.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32171151/

** "Domain adaptation strategies for 3D reconstruction of the lumbar spine using real fluoroscopy data" (2024), Medical Image Analysis
- FLAG: METADATA-only (abstract; not fetched in full)
- CycleGAN-style adaptation between real fluoroscopy and synthetic/DRR domain for 3D reconstruction. Corroborates that unlabeled real target data + adaptation is the standard de-risking tool when synthetic realism is limited.
- URL: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.media.2024.103322

* Findings
** Full 6-DOF regression is dead at N=3 — CONFIRMED, and the number is worse than "a lot"
- The one published deep pose-regression system for knee implants in single-plane fluoroscopy consumed ~106,000 annotated images (Vogl 2022). FoundationPose-style models are trained at foundation scale (Wen 2024). Regression on 3 frames is not a scaling question, it is a different problem class. [Confidence: high]
- Relevance: the repo's DIRECT optimizer over the GPU cost is the right tool for pose search; learning would not replace it.

** The ML NO-GO verdict survives its own paper's data — with a sharper reason
- Jensen 2024's ML classifiers classify KINEMATICS SEQUENCES (pose-domain features),
  trained on ~13,000 human-supervised sequences, scoring 88–94% internal. The pure
  spline/continuity method — no learning — scores 91.1% accuracy / 94% specificity on the
  same task. The learned edge over the analytic continuity measurement is a few points at
  best, and it cost 13k labels. [Confidence: high]
- This is direct, same-task evidence for the owner's 2026-08-12 verdict: the
  psi/continuity measurement is the cheap transfer; the classifier is not the value.
- Relevance: the repo's SymTrap directive + Jensen's dyad/spline machinery already
  delivers ~the same accuracy the ML classifiers do, with zero data.

** Binary classification is data-cheap where classes are visually distinct — but the mirror trap is the worst case for that
- Karnuta 2021: 9-class implant-ID CNN from 682 real radiographs → AUC 0.99. Classification on X-ray images demonstrably works at ~10²–10³ real labels. [Confidence: high]
- But the mirror decision is between near-identical silhouettes: Jensen 2024's own ML drops to 71% accuracy within 5° of pure-lateral (vs 88% beyond). The image carries least information exactly where the classifier is asked to decide. That regime is where the analytic dyad construction is exact and a learned boundary is noise-fitting. [Confidence: high]
- Relevance: "binary needs less data than regression" is true in general but does not rescue an image-domain mirror classifier, because the hard cases are image-degenerate.

** Synthetic DRR training is real and evidence-backed — but the realism ladder is the whole game
- DeepDRR (2018): naive-DRR-trained models fail on clinical fluoroscopy; realistically-simulated training transfers without adaptation. SyntheX (2024): realistic sim + domain randomization ≈ real-data training on hip fluoroscopy (7.59 vs 6.90 mm landmark error); naive sim degrades. [Confidence: high]
- Critically for this repo: metal is NOT modeled in the cited realistic simulators (DeepDRR lists metal as future work). The repo's renderer is CAD-silhouette-grade (naive-DRR-class), at ~7k DRRs/s. Training a pixel-domain classifier on it reproduces the documented DeepDRR failure mode; metal attenuation/beam-hardening/scatter — the exact physics that makes implants hard — is absent from both the renderer and the literature's simulators. [Confidence: high on the DeepDRR/SyntheX claims; the renderer's physical fidelity is INFERRED from local recon]
- Relevance: the synthetic-data reframe does NOT flip the NO-GO for image-domain learning until the renderer is upgraded to realistic simulation (material decomposition, spectra, scatter, metal), which is a medical-physics project, not an ML project.

** Symmetry-aware losses are training-time machinery, not runtime disambiguators — and the repo has no trainable network in the runtime seam
- ADD-S (PoseCNN, 2018), EPOS one-to-many correspondences (2020), Implicit-PDF continuous-symmetry densities (2021), multi-pose VSD-inspired losses trained on synthetic-only data (Bengtson 2021), and the closed-loop representation requirement (Richter-Klug & Frese 2021) all make TRAINING robust to symmetric pose copies. None of them returns "the true pose" at runtime: every one of them still needs a selection/argmax/disambiguation policy over the hypotheses — which is precisely the Jensen binary decision. [Confidence: high]
- FoundationPose at test time still flips between valid symmetric orientations (issues #395/#107) — the deep-pose family treats discrete symmetry as an open, user-handled problem. The repo's analytic dyad directive is more complete on this axis than FoundationPose. [Confidence: high]
- Relevance: symmetry-aware losses would only matter if the repo adopts a trainable pose hypothesis generator. They cannot replace the dyad; they would sit upstream of it and inherit the same disambiguation problem.

** Evidence floor for "synthetic mirror classifier beats the analytic dyad": NOT MET
- No published work trains a binary mirror/trap classifier on synthetic DRRs for implant disambiguation (searched; see What Could Not Be Verified). The nearest evidence: (a) Jensen 2024 shows ML's edge over analytic continuity is small and came from 13k real labels; (b) DeepDRR/SyntheX show pixel-domain synthetic training requires realistic simulation; (c) Karnuta shows classification is cheap only when classes are visually distinct. [Confidence: high on each cited claim; the absence claim is INFERRED from search coverage]
- Where analytic wins: the near-degenerate ≤5° regime (Jensen's ML: 71%) and zero-data deployments; it is also protocol-integrable (Jensen's oblique-view imaging protocol).
- Where learned could win (unproven): if a pose-domain (not pixel-domain) classifier on top of dyad candidates + continuity features could beat the spline on trajectory-level decisions using UNLIMITED synthetic pose pairs (ground truth known by construction) plus ~3 real frames for sanity-checking. That is the only formulation where the sample-side objection dissolves. [Confidence: medium — formulation sound, zero direct evidence]

** Deployment shapes
- In-engine C++ (ONNX Runtime C++ API / TensorRT engine): documented, standard, microseconds for a small classifier on the 3090 Ti — compute is not the constraint; training pipeline, weight provenance, and R12/R15 behavior preservation are. The repo's existing .pt-env-var torch test surface (JTML_SEG_PT / JTML_FEM_ESTIMATE_PT) is the ready-made seam for optional-weight artifacts. [Confidence: high on tooling; INFERRED on repo seam from local recon]
- Python/scikit post-processing: exactly what Jensen shipped (ML classifiers + spline post-processing on kinematics). Acceptable for research; a second runtime for a Qt6/C++ product. [Confidence: high]
- None: remains the evidence-backed default — Jensen 2024's own spline-only path reaches 91.1% accuracy with zero learned components. [Confidence: high]

- Whether ANY published system trains a mirror/trap disambiguator on synthetic DRRs (pixel or pose domain): no such work surfaced across the search set; absence is claimed only to the coverage of those searches.
- The physical fidelity of the repo's DRR renderer (attenuation model, spectra, scatter, metal handling, anatomy inclusion): not verifiable from outside the repo; local recon describes a CAD-silhouette renderer at ~7k DRRs/s. Whether it is "naive-DRR-grade" is an inference from the DeepDRR/SyntheX taxonomy, not a measurement.
- SyntheX's full author list (paper verified in full text via PMC; authorship not re-verified in this run).
- Whether the 3 labeled frames' self-referential labels (same STL at fitted poses) would poison a pose-domain classifier's training: plausibly yes per Jensen 2023's label-generation loop, but no direct quantitative evidence was retrieved.
- Rad et al.'s symmetry-classifier detail (quoted via Manhardt 2019) — the CVPR 2018 full-text extraction returned a stub.

* Implications for JTML (concrete)
1. Verdict on the NO-GO: UPHELD, but restated precisely. Image-domain learning for sym-trap is NO-GO on sim-to-real grounds (renderer is naive-DRR-class; metal unmodeled in the simulators that do transfer), not merely on data-scale grounds. Pose-domain learning (Jensen's formulation) is feasible in principle with unlimited synthetic pose pairs but is unproven to beat the analytic spline/continuity path — and Jensen's own numbers show the learned edge is small. Regression (FoundationPose/PVNet/deep surveys) is irrelevant to the runtime seam at any data scale.
2. The one learned experiment worth running (research-only, zero runtime risk): a synthetic-data ABLATION that scores the repo's dyad/continuity directive against a synthetic-trained oracle on DRR-registration outputs (pose-domain features; ground truth known by construction; labels NOT from the 3 real frames, so the loop-closure label problem is avoided). This uses the renderer's 7k DRRs/s, reuses the .pt-env-var optional-weight test seam, and answers "does the analytic choice match the data-best choice" without shipping any learned component. It is a measurement artifact, not a product component — consistent with R12/R15 and the pure jtml_domain seam.
3. If a learned component is ever wanted at runtime, the evidence-backed shape is: pose-domain binary classifier (Jensen-style features: dyad gap, continuity/spline residual, ambiguity-zone flags) exported ONNX → TensorRT engine in C++, weights shipped as an optional artifact behind the existing env-var test pattern. NOT a pixel-domain CNN, and NOT Python in the product path.
4. Do not spend effort on symmetry-aware losses or deep pose networks for this repo: they are training-time machinery for a network that does not exist in the runtime seam, and FoundationPose et al. demonstrably fail to solve the exact disambiguation the repo already solves analytically.
5. Cheapest real transfer (unchanged from the 2026-08-12 verdict, now with same-task published numbers): the Jensen 2024 spline/continuity + oblique-view imaging-protocol guidance. Jensen's own data quantifies it at ~91% accuracy with 0 labels; the ML classifiers add points, not a regime change.
