#+TITLE: Angle 02 — Post-Optimization Symmetry Disambiguation: From Dyads to Orbits to Diffusion
#+DATE: 2026-08-14
#+PANOPTES_ANGLE: 02-02-symmetry-disambiguation

* Summary
The literature decisively supports the repo's architectural bet: the proven fix for symmetric-implant ambiguity in single-plane fluoroscopy is **post-optimization disambiguation, not cost redesign** — Jensen 2024 (J Biomech) constructs "symmetry trap" dyads from a true pose and disambiguates with classifiers plus a sequence-level spline; Hermans 2007 already treated the tibial tray's two near-identical projection poses statistically; and the repo's own `compute_mirror_pose` + `tibial_pose_selector` is exactly this pattern, code-verified. The decisive design axis is **symmetry-group type**: a tibial tray has ONE discrete dual pose (mirror dyad — what the repo handles), whereas a hip stem, acetabular cup, or glenosphere has a CONTINUOUS rotation orbit where dyad selection is meaningless — the field's fix there is to remove the unobservable DOF via a canonical/quotient parameterization (e.g., the cup ellipse paper drops the polar-axis roll entirely). SE(3) score-based diffusion (Hsiao 2024) is elegant for RGB multi-modality but has **zero published evidence on monoplane metallic silhouettes**, carries high sampling cost, and does not remove the need for final disambiguation — do not adopt now.

** Q2 — How general is dyad/mirror-pose generation across symmetry groups?
- **Discrete mirror (tibial tray, acetabular cup rim landmarks in-plane)**: exactly TWO poses give near-identical sagittal projections (Hermans 2007, VERIFIED-abstract; Jensen 2024, VERIFIED-abstract; JTML, VERIFIED-full-text). The dyad is analytically constructible once a pose and the view geometry are known — this is precisely the repo's `compute_mirror_pose` (Crane-Duffy dyad, code-verified). BOP-Distrib generalizes this: for objects with an EXACT symmetry, the full solution set is a precomputable transform set applied to any recovered pose (VERIFIED-full-text).
- **Continuous rotational orbit (stem, cup, glenosphere)**: the "dual pose" is not a single pose but a 1-parameter SO(2) orbit (rotation about the symmetry axis leaves the projection invariant). Dyad construction is geometrically meaningless here — the field's fix is quotienting: the cup paper simply excludes the polar-axis roll from the parameterization (VERIFIED-full-text, arXiv 2503.07763v1: 5-parameter pose (θ,φ,k,l,h), roll never estimated). For a spherical head it would be an SO(3) orbit. **What breaks**: the mirror-machinery's assumption of "one other pose" breaks; what survives is canonicalization (report the orbit's canonical representative) plus explicit orbit reporting.
- **Near-symmetric-but-not-exact**: this is the Jensen trap regime — the tray is NOT exactly symmetric; the two poses project nearly identically only because the distinguishing features are weak. Disambiguation must be evidence-based (cost comparison, context, classifier). Critically, trap strength is **view-dependent**: JTML defined an "ambiguous zone" of apparent tibial varus/valgus < 3.6° capturing ~85% of blunders; Jensen 2024 reports ML accuracy of 71% within 5° of a pure-lateral view vs 88% beyond (VERIFIED-abstract). Suter et al. 2020 make the same point for bones: "different types of symmetry exist for different types of bone… the viewing angles that provide the least ambiguity… are not always obvious" [METADATA-only, snippet].

** Q3 — When is disambiguation ANALYTIC vs evidence-based?
- **Analytic (closed-form, like `compute_mirror_pose`)**: whenever the implant has an exact, known symmetry and a pose is available — the symmetry transform (mirror or orbit generator) is a fixed geometric object (BOP-Distrib ε-symmetry precomputation; repo dyad). Also analytic: quotient parameterizations that remove the DOF at modeling time (cup ellipse paper, VERIFIED-full-text). No evidence is needed because the equivalence class is exact.
- **Evidence-based (cost comparison, classifier, diffusion)**: whenever the equivalence is only approximate or view-induced — Jensen 2024 (ML classifiers + spline over kinematics sequences), Hermans 2007 (statistical), FoundationPose (learned hypothesis ranking over render-and-compare), cINN (learned multi-modal distribution). Jensen's headline numbers: classifiers 88-94% (internal) / 91-93% (external); spline algorithm 91.1% accuracy, 94% specificity, 67% sensitivity (VERIFIED-abstract).
- **Repo implication**: the shipped architecture is already correct on this axis — exact-ish mirror → analytic dyad; tie-break → evidence (varus/valgus vs femur, cost comparison in the sweep). The literature adds two things the repo lacks: (a) sequence-level (temporal) disambiguation for per-frame failures, and (b) an explicit "ambiguity flag" output (classify as obviously-true vs potentially-ambiguous).

** Q4 — Does SE(3) score-based diffusion offer anything for monoplane metallic silhouettes?
- **What Hsiao 2024 (CVPR) actually is**: score-based diffusion on the SE(3) Lie group, conditioned on a single **RGB** image; uses **no 3D model and no symmetry annotations**; closed-form Stein scores on SO(3) and R³×SO(3), surrogate score on SE(3) with sub-stepping; ResNet image encoder run once per image, MLP denoising; evaluated on SYMSOL/SYMSOL-T (5 textureless symmetric shapes) and T-LESS (30 objects with discrete and continuous symmetries), reporting BOP MSPD/MSSD/VSD plus R@2/5/10, T@2/5/10; generates 1,000 samples for density visualization [VERIFIED-full-text]. Foundational machinery: Riemannian score-based generative modeling (De Bortoli, NeurIPS 2022) and SO(3) diffusion (Yim et al.).
- **Transferability verdict (harsh)**: no published work applies any of this to X-ray/fluoroscopy, let alone monoplane metallic silhouettes. The RGB likelihood in Hsiao is a learned image-embedding condition; the fluoroscopy likelihood is a render-and-compare cost over a DRR — the repo's DIRECT global optimizer already evaluates that cost at ~143 μs/eval (7k evals/s), which is far cheaper than any learned score network plus reverse-process sampling (1000s of denoising steps per sample in the NCSN formulation). Additionally, diffusion does NOT remove the disambiguation problem: the sampled distribution remains multi-modal at the symmetry trap, so you still need the same dyad/selector/spline logic downstream. Cost/accuracy on metallic silhouettes is unquantified anywhere in the literature — a gap, not a benefit.
- **Where diffusion-type thinking could still help the repo**: as a *conceptual* model for "ambiguity = multi-modality of the pose posterior" (e.g., reporting the dyad cost-gap as a 2-mode posterior), not as an adopted algorithm.

** Q5 — Hips/shoulders: ambiguity rates, DOF affected, successful disambiguation
- **Shoulder**: Zhu et al. 2012 (Med Eng Phys) compared single- vs dual-plane fluoroscopic 2D-3D registration for shoulder kinematics; the abstract (METADATA-only — publisher elided the abstract via Semantic Scholar; snippet-level claim from orthoarchives) demonstrates accurate and repeatable kinematics with **dual-plane**, and the field moved to biplane for shoulders precisely because single-plane is weak. Single-plane shoulder-complex validation (PMC5803407) reports errors up to roughly 4.2 mm and 3.3-3.4° across joints [VERIFIED-abstract, snippet-level numbers]. rTSA glenosphere pose is estimated in practice by model-image silhouette registration (PMC8178639, METADATA) — the glenosphere is a hemisphere (continuous SO(2) orbit), so the same quotient logic as the cup applies; no published "ambiguity rate" was found for shoulder components.
- **Hip**: the acetabular cup's rotation about its polar axis is structurally unobservable from any single view of a hemisphere — the ellipse-fitting paper (VERIFIED-full-text) achieves θ 0.27°/φ 0.61° MAE (synthetic), 1.53-2.16° and 1.12-9.76 mm (implant CT), ~1.2-1.4 s, CPU-only, by parameterizing only the observable DOF. Clinical cup-orientation measurement from AP radiographs is similarly 2-DOF (inclination/anteversion) with well-documented systematic errors from X-ray beam offset and pelvic orientation (HipMatch, PMC3046689, METADATA). The femoral stem: rotation about the stem axis (version) is the weakly constrained DOF; radiological version measurement exists (e.g., Schmotzer & Learmonth 1988; a novel AP-radiograph method validated vs CT in PubMed 25737512, both METADATA) precisely because the **neck breaks the cylinder symmetry** — i.e., near-symmetric-but-not-exact, where evidence-based/geometric disambiguation works, not because the stem is exactly cylindrical.
- **Knee (the only quantified case)**: JTML (VERIFIED-full-text): 43 tibial blunders / 392 images; **66% of tibial blunders attributed to symmetry ambiguities**; 11% blunder rate inside the ambiguous zone (<3.6° apparent varus/valgus) vs 3.2% outside. This is the best available "ambiguity rate" in the implant-registration literature and it is exactly the regime the repo's SymTrap was built for.

* Key Findings
** F1 — Post-hoc disambiguation is the field's validated fix for implant symmetry; cost redesign is not the primary lever.
Jensen 2024 (post-processing algorithm + ML + spline), Hermans 2007 (statistical solution
selection), JTML (tibia-after-femur ordering + penalties) all treat disambiguation as an
explicit post-registration/registration-adjacent step. [Source:
https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1016/j.jbiomech.2024.112172;
https://mlanthology.org/iccv/2007/hermans2007iccv-statistical/;
https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.00057] Confidence: **high**. Relevance: validates
the repo's shipped SymTrap direction against the user belief that "cost functions don't
provide signal."
** F2 — The repo already ships the analytic dyad + context-based selection for the knee, matching Jensen's mechanism but simpler.
Code-verified: `compute_mirror_pose` (Crane-Duffy dyad: axis M = z_ax × view, rotation 2·acos(z_ax·view)) and `tibial_pose_selector` (choose the dyad member with lower varus/valgus relative to the femur — the tibia-after-femur context prior). Jensen 2024 goes further with 8 ML classifiers + temporal splines (91.1% spline accuracy); the repo's per-frame deterministic VV rule is a defensible minimal version of the same idea. [Sources: repo code; Jensen abstract] Confidence: **high** (code + abstract verified). Relevance: the gap between repo and Jensen-2024 is sequence-level disambiguation, not dyad generation.
** F3 — The symmetry-group type is decisive: discrete dual pose (knee tray) vs continuous orbit (stem/cup/glenosphere) require different machinery.
The cup literature (VERIFIED-full-text) does not disambiguate an orbit — it removes the unobservable polar-axis roll from the parameter space (5-DOF (θ,φ,k,l,h)) and reports only what the image can constrain. A cylindrical stem's version is only measurable because the neck breaks cylinder symmetry (METADATA radiological-version literature). The repo's dyad engine therefore does **not** transfer directly to hip/shoulder: it must be replaced by canonicalization + orbit reporting. [Sources: https://arxiv.org/html/2503.07763v1; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25737512/; https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1243/EMED_JOUR_1988_017_005_02] Confidence: **high** for the group distinction, **medium** for "dyad engine unusable as-is" (inference from geometry). Relevance: hips/shoulders need a symmetry-group-aware dispatcher, not a bigger dyad.
** F4 — Per-frame evidence-based disambiguation degrades near pure-lateral views; sequence context and view geometry are the proven remedies.
Jensen 2024: classifier accuracy 71% within 5° of pure-lateral vs 88% beyond; the imaging-protocol recommendation (more oblique views) is a published, actionable mitigation; JTML's ambiguous zone (<3.6° varus/valgus) quantifies the danger region. [Sources: Jensen abstract; JTML full text] Confidence: **high** for reported numbers, **medium** for transferability to the repo's exact fixtures. Relevance: the repo's 60-pose sweep + pin relay already surfaces per-pose cost around the optimum — the cost gap between dyad members is a natural ambiguity signal, and a coordinator-level sequence pass (Jensen-spline analog) is the highest-value new capability.
** F5 — SE(3) diffusion has no published evidence on monoplane metallic silhouettes; sampling is expensive; multi-modality persists.
Hsiao 2024 is RGB-only, model-free, annotation-free, on SYMSOL/T-LESS; diffusion at 1000s of denoising sub-steps per sample is not competitive with the repo's 143 μs/eval DIRECT cost evaluation; and the sampled posterior still contains the symmetry trap modes, so dyad/selector logic remains necessary. [Sources: https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/html/Hsiao_Confronting_Ambiguity_in_6D_Object_Pose_Estimation_via_Score-Based_Diffusion_CVPR_2024_paper.html; https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15873] Confidence: **high** (modality facts), **medium** (non-transferability is an inference from absence). Relevance: do not adopt; document as a monitored gap.
** F6 — Ambiguity is view-dependent, not a fixed property of the implant.
JTML ambiguous zone; BOP-Distrib's whole contribution is per-image ε-symmetry (visibility-dependent equivalence sets, precomputable only for exact global symmetries); Jensen's oblique-view protocol; Suter 2020 (bone-specific symmetry/view guidance). [Sources: https://arxiv.org/html/2408.17297v2; https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.00057; https://shapemodelling.cs.unibas.ch/gravis-site-archive/publications/2020/2020_Thusini_UncertaintyReductionInContour.pdf] Confidence: **high**. Relevance: the repo should emit an explicit ambiguity metric per frame (dyad cost gap + apparent varus/valgus) as standard pose-record output — currently implicit in the sweep relay.
** F7 — For exact-symmetry implants, the equivalence class is analytic and precomputable (BOP-Distrib principle); evidence is only needed for approximate/view-induced equivalence.
This is the formal justification for `compute_mirror_pose`: the mirror transform is a fixed geometric object applied to any recovered pose, matching "pre-computed offline and applied to the initial solution" (BOP-Distrib). [Sources: https://arxiv.org/html/2408.17297v2; repo code] Confidence: **high**. Relevance: analytic-first design is literature-aligned; reserve learned/evidence-based machinery for the near-symmetric and sequence-level cases.

* Implications for JTML (concrete, R15-safe)
** Already shipped (do not rebuild):
1. Analytic dyad construction — `compute_mirror_pose` (sym_trap_functions.cpp:11-101), Crane-Duffy mirror about the view axis. Matches the literature's "precomputed symmetry pattern applied to the recovered pose."
2. Context-based tie-break — `tibial_pose_selector` (ambiguous_pose_processing.cpp:9-20): lower-varus/valgus dyad member relative to femur; this IS the tibia-after-femur ordering.
3. Analysis sweep — `CalculateSymTrap` (optimizer_manager.cpp:1324+): 60-pose cost sweep around the optimum, per-pose UI relay (pin relay=61), zero search costCalls under the no-search Sym_Trap leaf. NOTE: do not conflate this DOMAIN directive with the COMPUTE `sym_trap_function` cost (pole distance + varus/valgus penalty asin(fem2tib[2][1]), weights 75/500) — two different features sharing a name.
** New (ranked, each landing in existing seams, pure domain-layer math where possible):
1. **Symmetry-group-aware dispatch (the generalization the angle demands).** Add a per-implant-class symmetry descriptor — {none | discrete dual | SO(2) orbit | SO(3) orbit} — and dispatch in the domain layer (pure Qt/GPU-free; the planned Rust-interop surface). Discrete dual → existing `compute_mirror_pose` + selector. Continuous orbit → canonical-frame output + explicit orbit report; NO dyad selection (F3). This is a small classifier function, not an abstraction layer (R12: build the orbit branch only when hip/shoulder models actually land).
2. **Emit ambiguity as data.** Standardize an ambiguity record on the pose output: dyad cost gap (sweep already computes both members' costs), apparent varus/valgus angle (already computed), view-angle-vs-lateral metric. This makes the view-dependence (F6) and the 71%-vs-88% regime (F4) observable per frame; no new search, R15-safe (pure data).
3. **Sequence-level disambiguation (Jensen-spline analog) — the highest-value new capability.** Coordinator-level directive (stage-script registry seam, plan 009): over a frame sequence, classify frames as "obviously true" (large dyad cost gap / outside ambiguous zone) vs "potentially ambiguous"; fit a smooth pose spline through obvious frames; reorient ambiguous frames to the spline-consistent dyad member. Published backing: Jensen 2024 spline (91.1% accuracy / 94% specificity) [VERIFIED-abstract]. Pure domain math, headless-testable, no GPU (R15).
4. **View-geometry hint.** Reuse the relay pattern to surface "frame in ambiguous zone; oblique view >5° recommended" (Jensen's imaging protocol). Zero new machinery.
5. **Do NOT adopt SE(3) diffusion.** Record the decision + the monitored gap (no fluoroscopy evidence; sampling cost ≫ 143 μs/eval; multi-modality persists) in the synthesis. Revisit only if (a) a published diffusion/RGB-D-type method demonstrates metallic-silhouette gains, or (b) a fluoroscopy-likelihood diffusion with viable sampling appears.
6. **Flag for other angles:** the cup's 5-DOF ellipse parameterization (arXiv 2503.07763) is a geometry-side option for hip (feeds angle 01/03/05); learned mirror-pair classification is angle 04's territory; the continuous-vs-discrete group taxonomy lives in angle 05 (cited here, not built here).

Boundary note: everything above is pose-space disambiguation given a cost (my scope). How the cost landscape is searched is angle 03's; the same 60-pose sweep data may feed both, but the sweep is disambiguation evidence here.
