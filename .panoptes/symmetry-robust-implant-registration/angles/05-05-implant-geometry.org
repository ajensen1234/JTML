* Summary

'Near-symmetric implant' is not one problem: it is a family of *different* symmetry
problems. A spherical femoral/humeral head is fully isotropic (O(3)); a hemispherical
acetabular cup / glenosphere is C∞v (continuous spin about its polar axis plus mirror
planes, no equatorial mirror); a femoral stem is strictly C1 but *near*-axisymmetric
(its trapezoidal/quadrangular cross-sections and CCD neck are exactly the features that
break the spin degeneracy); an all-polyethylene glenoid is radiographically nearly
invisible; and the knee tibial tray (the repo's solved case) has a *discrete* mirror
ambiguity (2 poses), not a continuum. The distinction that governs every other angle:
**discrete symmetry → enumerable ambiguity → post-processing can fix it (SymTrap works);
continuous symmetry → a 1-parameter manifold of image-equivalent poses → an information
floor that no silhouette/contour cost, and no biplane pair, can lift.** The cup's
rotation about its polar axis is documented in the hip-kinematics literature as
"cannot be uniquely determined" from contours (D'Isidoro & Ferguson 2018; D'Isidoro et
al. 2023), and the state-of-the-art cup-ellipse registration methods parameterize the
cup with only 5 DOF, silently omitting the spin (Suh et al. 2025). The stem's
internal-external rotation (spin about its long axis) is *weakly conditioned*, not
floored: measured monoplane RMS ~2.0° vs 1.2-1.4° for the other rotations at a 45°
oblique view (D'Isidoro 2017, cited in 2023). Oblique single views are the one
data-acquisition lever that improves both stem-spin conditioning and knee symmetry-trap
disambiguation (Jensen 2024).

* Accountable research questions

The five questions this angle answers:

1. For each hip/shoulder implant class (femoral stem, humeral head + humeral stem,
   acetabular cup, glenosphere/baseplate, glenoid, plus reverse-shoulder humeral cup):
   what is the *exact* symmetry group — continuous rotational about which axis, discrete
   N-fold, mirror planes, or none?
2. What features BREAK each symmetry (neck/CCD, taper, collar, fins/ribs, cross-section
   shape, screw holes, offset, version, coating boundaries), and are they visible in a
   monoplane X-ray projection — as silhouette, as shading/edge, or not at all?
3. For the worst cases (spherical head, cylindrical stem, hemispherical cup): is there
   ANY single-plane projection evidence that can disambiguate rotation about the
   symmetry axis, or is the DOF fundamentally unobservable (information floor)? What
   about biplane?
4. Near-symmetric vs exactly symmetric in practice: do real implants carry
   manufacturing asymmetry (machined features, logos, serial numbers) that X-ray can
   pick up?
5. How do the tibial tray (already solved by the repo's SymTrap) and the femur compare,
   to anchor the knee→hip/shoulder mapping?

* Findings

** Finding 1 — The taxonomy: hips/shoulders are three DIFFERENT symmetry problems, not
   one 'near-symmetric' class

- Class A (isotropic point-like): the spherical femoral/humeral head. Symmetry group
  O(3) (every rotation about the center). No unique axis exists. ANALYTIC-reasoning +
  ASTM F2033 (heads manufactured to sphericity spec). No single view, no pair of views,
  can recover rotation about *any* axis through the head center from the head's own
  image — the head's rotation DOFs are only recoverable because the head is rigidly
  Morse-tapered to the asymmetric stem, so its pose is inherited from the stem.
- Class B (continuous axisymmetric): the acetabular cup and glenosphere. Ideal group
  C∞v (hemisphere): continuous rotation about the polar axis, ∞ vertical mirror planes,
  NO equatorial mirror (the dome breaks it). The polar-axis direction (2 rotation DOF =
  inclination + anteversion) is recoverable from the projected rim ellipse; the spin
  about the polar axis (1 DOF) is an information floor for any contour/silhouette cost.
  VERIFIED: D'Isidoro & Ferguson 2018 (explicit); Suh et al. 2025 (5-DOF parameterization);
  PMC9813239 (clinical practice measures only inclination + anteversion).
- Class C (strictly asymmetric, near-axisymmetric): femoral/humeral stems. Exact group
  C1 (neck/CCD breaks everything); approximate axis = the canal/long axis. The
  deviation from axisymmetry (cross-section, neck, CCD, collar, fins) is exactly what
  makes spin about the long axis recoverable in principle. Weakly conditioned, not
  floored: monoplane IE RMS ~2.0° vs 1.2-1.4° for other rotations (D'Isidoro 2017
  numbers cited in 2023 Frontiers).
- Class D (discrete mirror): the knee tibial tray (repo baseline) — approx Cs, exactly
  two image-equivalent poses → SymTrap/Crane-Duffy dyad works. Glenoid dish is closer
  to Cs than to any rotational group but is nearly invisible radiographically.
- Class E (invisible): the all-PE glenoid and the reverse humeral cup liner —
  radiolucent; the tracking signal is bone/cement or metal markers.

Confidence: high (literature + analytic).

** Finding 3 — The information floor is real, documented, and parameterized away

- Cup: "rotation around the symmetry axis can not be uniquely determined with the sole
  use of contours" (D'Isidoro & Ferguson 2018, VERIFIED-full-text); the 2023 follow-up
  seeds that DOF from skin markers (VERIFIED-full-text); the 2025 differentiable
  ellipse method simply omits it from the pose model (VERIFIED-full-text). The clinical
  measurement literature measures cup inclination + anteversion only (PMC9813239,
  VERIFIED-full-text) — the spin is never measured clinically either.
- Sphere: no projection of a sphere carries rotation information about the center —
  the silhouette is a circle and the transmission image is rotation-invariant for every
  axis through the center. ANALYTIC-reasoning; heads manufactured to sphericity spec
  (ASTM F2033).
- Cylinder: silhouette invariant to spin about its axis by definition of rotational
  symmetry (ANALYTIC-reasoning). Real stems escape this because no commercial stem is a
  perfect cylinder: cross-sections are trapezoid/quadrangular (CORAIL), tapered both
  planes (Exeter), or flare proximally (AML). The AML's distal segment is the closest
  to a true cylinder and contributes the least spin signal.
- Biplane: resolves the discrete *circle-pose* 2-fold ambiguity of a projected rim
  ellipse (which rim orientation is the true one) and over-determines the polar axis
  and the stem's spin (two aspect silhouettes see the cross-section asymmetry from two
  directions) — but a biplane pair of an *exactly* C∞v hemisphere still sees no spin
  signal: both views are invariant. Biplane strengthens conditioning; it does not lift
  a continuous-symmetry floor. ANALYTIC-reasoning.
- Caveat for the repo's renderer: JTML renders the full mesh projection as a binary
  image, so *through-voids* (screw holes) can appear as holes in the rendered
  silhouette when the view is sufficiently en-face, and the real X-ray shows them as
  lucent spots — so screw holes are not strictly out of reach of the current cost in
  favorable poses; interior *density* features (coatings, taper lines) are.

Confidence: high (multiple verified sources + analytic).

** Finding 4 — Discrete vs continuous ambiguity: what SymTrap can and cannot generalize

- Knee tibia: exactly 2 image-equivalent poses (the 'true' and the 'symmetry trap'),
  generated by reflection in the plane spanned by the implant z-axis and the view
  direction (Crane-Duffy construction, repo compute_mirror_pose). Finite set →
  enumerable → post-processing/ML classification is the right tool (Jensen 2024;
  repo's CalculateSymTrap sweep + dyad measurement).
- Cup spin / cylinder spin / head rotation: a 1-parameter (or 2-parameter for the
  sphere) *continuum* of equivalent poses. No post-processing can recover a DOF that
  never entered the image; the information must come from a different sensor modality
  (asymmetric feature in the same image, bone context, temporal/kinematic priors, or
  an external reference like skin markers as in D'Isidoro 2023).
- Angles 02 must therefore not over-generalize the dyad/mirror machinery to
  continuous-symmetry classes; the correct geometry for those classes is a
  symmetry-axis *parameterization* of the pose space (and reporting the unobservable
  subspace), which is a measurement/geometry statement, not a disambiguation step.

Confidence: high (analytic + Jensen 2024 + D'Isidoro).

** Finding 5 — 'The symmetric axis' is not well-defined per implant

- Head: no axis at all (isotropic).
- Cup/glenosphere: one well-defined polar axis (C∞v).
- Stems: no exact axis (C1); an approximate canal axis is a modeling convenience, and
  the deviation from axisymmetry is the entire source of spin signal — the 'axis' is
  where the signal is weakest, not where it is defined.
- Tibial tray/glenoid: mirror element, not a rotation axis.
- Projection subtlety (challenges the 'symmetric in 3D ⇒ symmetric in projection'
  assumption): the projection of a rotationally symmetric object is mirror-symmetric
  about the projected symmetry axis for every pose — so silhouette-based costs see the
  projected axis direction (e.g., the cup rim ellipse's minor axis) but are blind to
  the azimuthal angle around it. Conversely, a 3D-asymmetric object can project to a
  near-symmetric image for specific poses (a stem viewed exactly along its neck plane,
  a screw-hole pattern aligned with the view), so 'near-symmetric in projection' is a
  pose-dependent statement, not an object property. ANALYTIC-reasoning; the pose
  dependence is what oblique views (Jensen 2024) exploit.

Confidence: high.

** Finding 6 — Real implants do carry manufacturing asymmetry; only the shape-level
   part is radiographically usable

- Permanent marking of manufacturer/material/catalog/serial-lot is a standard practice
  (ASTM F983-86(2018)/F0983-24, VERIFIED-partial).
- Forensic identification and automated implant-ID systems identify make/model from
  plain radiographs at scale (PMC9335431; PMID 36940755, METADATA-only) — i.e.,
  manufacturer-distinct *shape* asymmetries (neck geometry, cross-sections, stem
  profiles, coating extents) are radiographically resolvable and could, in principle,
  participate in pose disambiguation.
- Serial-number-scale etchings/logos are NOT demonstrated as readable on clinical
  fluoroscopy in any source I verified → treat as unavailable for pose work (see
  What-Could-Not-Be-Verified).

** Finding 7 — Knee→hip/shoulder mapping anchor (question 5)

- The repo's femur (Lima KR, C1) is the asymmetric reference used for labeling; the
  tibia (approx Cs) is the ambiguous component with the discrete mirror trap — exactly
  the structure Jensen 2024 formalizes, and exactly what SymTrap's dyad measures.
- Knee femur ≈ Class C (asymmetric, near-axisymmetric condyles), knee tibia ≈ Class D
  (discrete mirror). Hip/shoulder: cup ≈ Class B (continuous), head ≈ Class A
  (isotropic), stem ≈ Class C. So the repo already handles one discrete-symmetry case
  well; the hip/shoulder classes that matter most (B, A) are *structurally different*
  — continuous, not discrete — which is why 'like the knee but rounder' is the wrong
  mental model (user-belief challenge #3 confirmed by literature).
- JTML's single rotation-sensitive prior (tibia varus/valgus penalty,
  sym_trap_function.cpp:132-134) is a discrete-mirror-breaking prior. Its hip analog
  would be priors on stem version (neck anteversion) and cup inclination/anteversion
  from planning, plus a declared-unknown cup spin.

Confidence: high.

* Implications for JTML (what angles 01-04 must respect)

Per class, which DOF are recoverable in monoplane silhouette terms and which are not:

1. **Acetabular cup (worst continuous case).** Recoverable: polar-axis orientation
   (inclination + anteversion) and 3 translations — the rim ellipse is a strong, cheap,
   analytic signal (Liaw method; Suh 2025 differentiable ellipse fitting, ~1.2-1.4 s
   CPU, sub-degree). NOT recoverable: spin about the polar axis — information floor.
   Angle 01 must not design a contour/shading cost that promises the spin; angle 02
   must not apply the dyad to it; angle 04 (learning) cannot learn it from monoplane
   data either (no label exists in the image — same floor). The spin must come from:
   (a) screw holes/rim features when visible (through-void signal is within reach of
   the existing silhouette renderer in favorable en-face poses), (b) pelvic-bone
   context (D'Isidoro 2018), (c) temporal/kinematic priors, or (d) be reported as
   unobservable (honest covariance). Biplane over-determines the polar axis but does
   not lift the spin floor.
2. **Femoral/humeral stem.** All 6 DOF recoverable; spin about the long axis is the
   weak one (monoplane RMS ~2.0° at 45° oblique vs 1.2-1.4° for the other two
   rotations). The cross-section (trapezoid/quadrangular), CCD neck, collar and fins
   are silhouette features — so a silhouette cost CAN see spin if the view is oblique;
   an AP-only view is the worst geometry. Data-acquisition lever (Jensen 2024 +
   D'Isidoro 2017): prefer 30-45° oblique views. This is the one class where 'near
   symmetric' reduces to a conditioning problem, not a floor.
3. **Spherical head.** No rotation signal of its own; treat as a landmark (circle
   center) and inherit rotation from the stem via the Morse taper. Do not give it
   rotation DOFs in the pose space (wasted DIRECT dimensions + noise).
4. **Glenoid (all-PE).** Invisible; registration must target bone (scapula) or metal
   markers. Any proposal that renders a glenoid STL as a silhouette source is
   physically wrong for radiographs. Metal-backed or marked variants only.
5. **Tibial tray (repo baseline).** Discrete mirror pair — SymTrap is the correct
   geometry; do not retrofit it to continuous cases.
6. **Cross-cutting.** (a) Do not assume a single 'symmetric axis' per implant — the
   pose space should be parameterized per class: cup = polar-axis + declared spin;
   stem = full SE(3) with weak-spin conditioning; head = 3 translations only. (b) The
   repo's cost sees envelope + through-voids, not interior density — feature inventories
   in angle 01 should be split by that visibility. (c) All projection-visibility claims
   in this angle are analytic/literature-based; the run has no measurement layer, so
   verifying them (e.g., a 'rotation-profile' sweep analog of jtml.z_profile: render
   silhouette-IoU vs spin angle for each class) requires the symmetry-probe
   instrumentation the user declined — flag any cost feature that depends on them as
   needing that instrumentation.
7. **Oblique-view guidance is a consensus across the literature** (Jensen 2024 for the
   knee, D'Isidoro 2017/2023 for the hip): a single oblique monoplane view beats an
   AP-only view for symmetry-adjacent DOFs and is the only sensor-side change that
   helps without new algorithms.
