* Summary

The repo's 7 registered cost variants are not 7 different metric families — they are
three ingredients (dilated-edge overlap, a mean-chamfer distance-map term, and geometric
priors) combined with different weights and switches, plus one Mahfouz-style intensity
term. The 2D-3D registration literature splits into four families: silhouette/edge
overlap + distance-transform (the implant/RSA mainstream: Mahfouz 2003, Flood & Banks
2017 D.R.E.C.T., model-based RSA), intensity-based (MI, NCC, pattern intensity),
gradient-based (gradient correlation, gradient difference, gradient orientation,
normalized gradient fields), and learned metrics (angle 03).

The dominant finding for JTML's weak axis: *every* rigorous monoplane study — Penney 1998
(source-to-film translation is the one axis excluded from the sub-0.54 mm/deg accuracy
claim even for the best metrics), Yamazaki 2004 (depth accuracy "relatively poor" because
a depth change produces only small silhouette changes), and the repo's own 6.31 mm z_trans
gap — reports the out-of-plane translation as the hardest axis regardless of metric. Metric
choice can improve z, but only terms that respond to *scale* (chamfer on the exact edge,
intensity gradients) or *shape* (curvature-weighted edges) can do it; dilated overlap is
almost flat in z because a ±15 mm z shift at ~1.1 m source distance changes magnification
by <1%, far inside the 6 px dilation tolerance.

The commented-out DistanceMapMetric term in src/compute/DIRECT_DILATION.cpp is dead code:
it is a literal duplicate of the already-active distance-map call (monoplane), not a
deliberate feature. Deleting it is safe; uncommenting it would silently double the chamfer
term (R13 behavior-delta risk).

* Alternatives

The landscape for 2D-3D registration of *metallic implants* to single-plane fluoroscopy,
with the repo's position on each family.

** A. Dilated-edge overlap (the repo's default family + D.R.E.C.T.)

- What it is: Canny/segmentation edge of the fluoro frame dilated by N px; rendered implant
  silhouette edge-detected and dilated by N px; asymmetric +1/-1 overlap count normalized
  by the comparison white-pixel sum. This is exactly FastImplantDilationMetric
  [src/compute/fast_implant_dilation_metric.cu] plus the precomputed white-sum
  [src/compute/DIRECT_DILATION.cpp:71-78].
- Provenance: the D.R.E.C.T. paper ("Automated Registration of 3-D Knee Implant Models to
  Fluoroscopic Images Using Lipschitzian Optimization", Flood & Banks, IEEE TMI 2017/2018,
  DOI 10.1109/TMI.2017.2773398) — "a pyramidal approach that identifies the correct pose
  by matching decreasing dilations of an edge-detected image with the silhouette of an
  implant model" (abstract, IEEE Xplore document/8106803). The repo's 6/3/1 stage dilation
  schedule [golden_oracle.org "Dilation settings (confirmed): trunk is 6px dilated, branch
  is 3px dilated, leaf is 1px dilated"] is that pyramid.
- Repo deviation from the paper: 5 cost files carry the comment "(DIFFERENT FROM JTA
  PAPER) Dilate rendered image to same dilation as comparison image"
  [src/compute/DIRECT_DILATION.cpp:71, DIRECT_DILATION_SAME_Z.cpp:80,104,
  DIRECT_DILATION_POLE_CONSTRAINT.cpp:113, sym_trap_function.cpp:177, DIRECT_DILATION_T1.cpp
  trunk path]. The repo dilates *both* sides to the same level; the paper per its abstract
  applies the dilation pyramid to the edge-detected fluoro image and matches against the
  (undilated) model silhouette. This deviation is documented but has never been ablated.
  Confidence: high on the repo side (source), medium on the paper side (paywalled; abstract
  only — see What Could Not Be Verified).
- Strengths: GPU-cheap (three kernels, integer atomics); dilation gives monotone coarse
  basins that suit a derivative-free DIRECT optimizer (which needs a bounded Lipschitz
  constant, not gradients — Jones et al. 1993 in refs/); validated end-to-end on the
  oracle (silhouette IoU 0.9936 vs 0.85 gate [test/golden/baseline.json:48,68]).
- Weaknesses:
  - Nearly flat in z: silhouette change under z-translation is pure magnification
    (scale-preserving), and the 6 px dilation tolerance swallows the <1% magnification
    change from the leaf's ±15 mm z range. This is the direct mechanism behind the
    6.31 mm z_trans gap [test/golden/baseline.json:61] and the leaf stage's purpose
    (Z_SEARCH_RANGE = Point6D(3,3,15,3,3,3) [include/domain/settings_constants.h:31]).
    Confidence: high (geometry + repo empirics; the 6.31 mm is measured, the magnification
    arithmetic is derived — see What Could Not Be Verified).
  - Asymmetric: normalized by comparison-white-sum, so a rendered edge that is *smaller*
    than the comparison (e.g. wrong z → smaller silhouette) is penalized less than one that
    sticks out. An IoU/Dice normalization (iou.cu already exists) would be symmetric.
  - Uses no interior intensity and no gradient strength: all information about the
    femoral notch, condylar geometry and implant pegs inside the silhouette is discarded —
    exactly the shape cues that disambiguate depth.
- Best suited when: the ML segmentation is clean, the budget is tight, and the task is
  in-plane pose (x, y, rotations); it is the correct default for trunk/branch capture.
- Variants in this family: DIRECT_DILATION (overlap + chamfer), DIRECT_DILATION_T1
  (see bug-4 below: rendered edge always dilated by 1), DIRECT_MAHFOUZ's contour term is
  a weighted-overlap variant (see family C).

** B. Distance-transform / chamfer family

- What it is: precompute a distance transform of the binary fluoro edge; score =
  mean/sum of transform values under the projected model edge. The repo's
  DistanceMapMetric [src/compute/distance_map_metric.cu] sums the CV_8UC1 L1 distance-map
  values under *exact EDGE_PIXELs* of the rendered image and divides by edge-pixel count —
  a mean chamfer distance. The distance map is built CPU-side from the Canny/segmentation
  edge: invert → cv::distanceTransform(DIST_L1, mask 5) [src/compute/frame.cu:113-122],
  uploaded as a GPUFrame [src/coordinator/optimizer_manager.cpp:682-689].
- Provenance: chamfer matching (Barrow 1977; Borgefors 1988 TPAMI 10(6):849-865 hierarchical
  chamfer; Borgefors 1986 showing sum-of-squared-distances reduces false minima — noted in
  "Hierarchical Chamfer Matching Based on Propagation of Gradient Strengths", ECCV 2006,
  https://doi.org/10.1007/11907350_26). The code comment says the metric exists to
  "convexify the search space a little bit more" vs the Hamming/dilation overlap
  [src/compute/distance_map_metric.cu:58-64].
- Strengths: the only z-sensitive term in the default cost — the exact-edge chamfer mean
  responds to scale mismatch that dilated overlap cannot see; classic, cheap, and the DT
  infra already exists in the repo. Confidence: high (code semantics) / medium (z-sensitivity
  is inferred from the mean-chamfer formulation, not measured — the ablation harness in
  Recommendations would measure it).
- Weaknesses: chamfer's classic failure — spurious edges in the DT source pull the optimum;
  here the DT source is the ML segmentation edge (clean when the model is good, unreliable
  when not; segmentation errors directly corrupt pose — Mahfouz 2005, J Biomech
  "Effect of segmentation errors on 3D-to-2D registration of implant models in X-ray
  images", https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15598449/). Mean-formulation is
  outlier-sensitive (a single spurious far edge adds a large value). It runs *after*
  FastImplantDilationMetric mutates the rendered image in place, so it sees the
  edge-dilated image and scores only the surviving border pixels
  [src/compute/DIRECT_DILATION.cpp:74-82 + fast_implant_dilation_metric.cu in-place kernels]
  — an unusual chamfer variant worth documenting.
- Scale mismatch: the overlap terms are pixel-count sums O(10^3-10^4); the chamfer term is
  a per-pixel mean O(1-10). Their blend weight is implicit and never calibrated.
- Best suited when: capture range / basin convexity matters more than soft-tissue
  robustness; used as the z-aware refinement term (leaf), not the capture term.

** C. Intensity-based (MI, NCC, pattern intensity) + Mahfouz's direct image-to-image

- What it is: compare the rendered projection's intensities to the fluoro intensities
  (DRR vs image) — MI (Viola-Wells 1997; Maes 1997), NCC, pattern intensity (Weese 1997;
  Penney 1998), and Mahfouz's direct image-to-image similarity "tak[ing] advantage of the
  speed of modern computer graphics workstations to quickly render simulated (predicted)
  images... does not require an accurate segmentation of the implant silhouette"
  (Mahfouz, Hoff, Komistek, Dennis, IEEE TMI 22(12):1561-1574, 2003,
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14649746/).
- Repo mapping: DIRECT_MAHFOUZ [src/compute/DIRECT_MAHFOUZ.cpp +
  src/compute/implant_mahfouz_metric.cu] implements Mahfouz's (Σ input×projected)/(Σ
  projected) intensity ratio over the silhouette *plus* an edge-energy overlap ratio
  (contour term, weight -2.67) — a faithful, GPU-ported Mahfouz cost. Its
  "Black_Silhouette" registered parameter is never read (dead config).
- Literature evidence: Penney 1998 — pattern intensity and gradient difference were the
  only two of six measures that stayed accurate and failure-free with soft tissue and
  instruments in the image; all parameters within 0.54 mm/deg rms *except the source-to-film
  (depth) translation* (IEEE TMI 17(4):586-595, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9845314/).
  Wu et al. 2009 (Med Phys 36(12):5391-403) — on kV phantom, NCC best accuracy+robustness
  but flat near the optimum (needs strict termination); on MV, PI/GD/GC best accuracy,
  NCC/NMI best robustness (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20095251/).
- Weaknesses: MI is "highly non-convex and has typically many local maxima" (Haber &
  Modersitzki, "Intensity gradient based registration and fusion of multi-modal images",
  Methods Inf Med 2007, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17354837/) — bad for a
  Lipschitzian global optimizer's assumptions; needs DRR intensity rendering (the repo has
  render_drr_engine.cu, but the registration path currently renders silhouettes); intensity
  matching is calibration-sensitive.
- Best suited when: the fluoro has real soft-tissue context and the DRR is trustworthy;
  intensity is what breaks the z ambiguity for bone (see "Evaluation of an intensity-based
  algorithm for 2D/3D registration of natural knee videofluoroscopy data", Med Eng Phys
  2020, https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1016/j.medengphy.2020.01.002 — knee-specific,
  feature-vs-intensity).

** D. Gradient-based (gradient correlation, gradient difference, gradient orientation, NGF)

- What it is: correlate gradient images instead of intensities; gradient magnitude
  normalizes illumination offsets. Family: gradient difference / gradient correlation
  (Penney 1998), normalized gradient fields (Droske & Rumpf 2004; Haber & Modersitzki 2007),
  gradient orientation (Otake 2016).
- Literature evidence: Otake et al. 2016, "3D-2D image registration for target localization
  in spine surgery: investigation of similarity metrics providing robustness to content
  mismatch" (Phys Med Biol 61(8):3009) — 51 CT / 115 intraoperative radiographs: with
  masking + 200 multistarts, GC and GO had 0% gross failures, median PDE < 6.4 mm;
  *without* masking at 50 multistarts GC failed >14% while GO stayed at 0% failures
  (median PDE 5.5 mm, 2.6 mm IQR, 29.3 s) (https://beta.iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/0031-9155/61/8/3009).
  So GO is the modern robustness pick; GC is strong but masking-dependent.
- Repo mapping: none of the 7 variants is gradient-based. The dead registration_metric.cu
  (fully commented out) contains the skeleton of an older gradient/edge metric.
- Weaknesses: for a metallic implant the DRR gradient is dominated by the implant's own
  silhouette — much of gradient-correlation's advantage over dilated overlap is recovered
  information about *internal* metal edges (femoral notch, peg holes) rather than soft
  tissue. Still monoplane-z-limited at the fundamental level (Penney 1998).
- Best suited when: content mismatch (instruments, soft tissue, bone overlap), natural-knee
  (non-implant) targets, and z-relevant internal structure.

** E. Learned / differentiable metrics (pointer only)

- CNN-based 2D-3D registration similarity has matured (e.g., Miao et al. 2016, "A CNN-based
  approach for 2D-3D registration", Med Image Anal; cf. also the Flood & Banks autonomous
  follow-up pipeline combining CNN segmentation + Fourier-descriptor initial pose +
  Lipschitzian refinement, postprint
  https://repository.uantwerpen.be/docstore/d:irua:17814). Per the run's angle-03 boundary,
  the learned-metric family is analyzed there; note for the synthesis: the repo already
  uses a CNN for segmentation, so a learned *similarity* would slot in at the same seam
  (segmentation output → cost), not as a replacement renderer.

** F. Geometric priors (the repo's constraint variants — not similarity metrics)

- These augment the overlap cost with regularization, and are the repo's existing answer
  to the z weakness:
  - DIRECT_DILATION_SAME_Z: adds Z_Weight·|z_fem − z_tib| [src/compute/DIRECT_DILATION_SAME_Z.cpp:86-88].
  - DIRECT_DILATION_POLE_CONSTRAINT: adds PoleWeight × distance from the femur to the plane
    spanned by the tibia's rotated in-plane axes [src/compute/DIRECT_DILATION_POLE_CONSTRAINT.cpp:100-117].
  - DD_NEW_POLE_CONSTRAINT: same plane-distance, decomposed per axis with X_TRANS/Y_TRANS/
    Z_TRANS switches [src/compute/DD_NEW_POLE_CONSTRAINT.cpp] — see bug-1/bug-2 below.
  - sym_trap_function: pole distance + varus/valgus angle penalty (vv = asin(fem2tib[2][1]),
    weight 500 vs pole weight 75) [src/compute/sym_trap_function.cpp:150-177].
- Critical scale finding: the overlap term is O(10^3-10^4); SAME_Z and
  DIRECT_DILATION_POLE_CONSTRAINT use weight 1 on mm-scale distances, so their penalties are
  ~0.01-0.3% of the base cost — effectively inert. sym_trap's weights (75/500) are the only
  ones in the right scale, which is likely why that variant was tuned. The per-axis
  selection booleans default to all-false (X_TRANS=false etc.), so DD_NEW_POLE_CONSTRAINT
  without explicit parameter setting degenerates toward plain DIRECT_DILATION (+ garbage,
  see bug-1). Confidence: high (scale arithmetic from source; medium on the "inert" claim
  until the harness measures it — a small penalty can still break DIRECT ties).
- Best suited when: a clinical prior is genuinely known (implant-to-implant geometry for
  TKA contact, varus/valgus bounds). They should be *calibrated weights*, not fixed 1s.

* Comparison on Decision Axes

- z_trans (depth) sensitivity: dilated overlap (all A-variants) is the flattest — z change
  is pure magnification, <1% over the leaf's ±15 mm, swallowed by 6 px dilation; the
  chamfer mean (B) responds to scale; intensity (C) and gradient (D) respond to
  magnification + internal structure. But the ceiling is structural: Penney 1998 excludes
  source-to-film translation from the sub-0.54 mm/deg claim even for pattern intensity and
  gradient difference [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9845314/]; Yamazaki 2004 states depth
  accuracy "is relatively poor because a change in the depth position causes only small
  changes in the 2-D silhouette" (IEEE TMI 23(5):602-619,
  https://researchr.org/publication/YamazakiWNSTYT04 — 198 citations); the repo's own
  measured 6.31 mm z_trans gap at a 15 mm informational tolerance
  [test/golden/baseline.json:53,61]. Repo variant ranking (expected): chamfer-heavy
  DIRECT_DILATION ≥ Mahfouz ≥ pure overlap; priors (F) only help if weighted correctly.
  Confidence: high on literature + repo empirics; the per-variant ranking is a hypothesis
  the harness must test.
- Robustness to soft tissue / contrast / instruments: pattern intensity and gradient
  difference win (Penney 1998); gradient orientation is the no-masking winner (Otake 2016);
  silhouette methods are *immune to intensity* but fragile to segmentation error and
  occlusion (Mahfouz 2005). For JTML the "soft tissue" is mostly handled upstream by the ML
  segmentation, so this axis matters mainly for segmentation failure modes.
- Sensitivity to the other pose axes (in-plane): x/y translation and z_rot are well served
  by overlap (large silhouette response); x_rot/y_rot are silhouette-shape axes with
  moderate response — this is where the dilation schedule (6/3/1) matters and where the
  paper's asymmetric schedule vs the repo's symmetric schedule (bug-4 fix + T1 ablation) is
  the open question.
- Optimizer compatibility (DIRECT/Lipschitzian, Jones 1993 in refs/): overlap metrics are
  integer, plateaued, and bounded — cheap Lipschitz bounds; the chamfer term convexifies
  (its stated purpose [distance_map_metric.cu comment]); MI is disqualified by
  non-convexity (Haber & Modersitzki 2007); gradient metrics are smooth but need per-call
  DRR gradient computation. The repo's DIRECT has no gradient support, which favors
  overlap/chamfer over gradient metrics for *cost-side* adoption.
- Integration cost in this repo: A and B variants need zero new infra (all kernels exist);
  the chamfer term needs only weight calibration; C needs DRR intensity rendering wired
  into the cost path (render_drr_engine.cu exists but is not used by the registration
  cost); D additionally needs gradient kernels; learned metrics need angle 03.
- Maintenance / correctness: 3 of 7 registered variants contain live bugs (bug-1 uninitialized
  read, bug-2 dead duplicate axis, bug-3 wrong transform argument) and one wiring bug makes
  T1's non-trunk path unreachable (bug-4); curvature_hausdorf_metric.cu returns 0 (stub);
  registration_metric.cu is commented-out dead code; INTENSITY_WEIGHT/EDGE_WEIGHT constants
  in settings_constants.h are unused. There is zero test coverage of metric math
  (test/unit/test_cost_function.cpp covers only the parameter registry).

* Bugs Found in the Registered Variants (all from source reading)

- bug-1 (UB): DD_NEW_POLE_CONSTRAINT declares `double min_dist;` uninitialized, then
  `min_dist += X_dist * pole_weight;` under `if (x_tran)` — an uninitialized read
  [src/compute/DD_NEW_POLE_CONSTRAINT.cpp:155-169]. With default all-false booleans the
  returned cost is `metric_score + garbage`.
- bug-2 (copy-paste): Y_dist is computed from the s-vector (rotated X-axis) instead of the
  r-vector (rotated Y-axis), so Y_dist ≡ X_dist exactly; the Y_TRANS constraint is a no-op
  duplicate of X_TRANS [src/compute/DD_NEW_POLE_CONSTRAINT.cpp:141-148 vs 133-139].
- bug-3 (wrong transform): sym_trap builds the tibia transform as
  create_312_transform(x2tib, p.z_location_, p.y_location_, p.z_location_, ...) — the x
  translation is replaced by z [src/compute/sym_trap_function.cpp:104-108 vs signature
  (transform, xt, yt, zt, zr, xr, yr) in include/compute/sym_trap_functionCustomVariables.h:56-59].
  The commented-out reference matrix above it uses p.x_location_ as intended. Corrupts the
  varus/valgus angle and pole distance computed from fem2tib.
- bug-4 (always-trunk): CostFunctionManager's (Stage) constructor guard
  `if (stage_ != Stage::Trunk || stage_ != Stage::Branch || stage_ != Stage::Leaf) stage_ = Stage::Trunk;`
  is always true [src/compute/CostFunctionManager.cpp:63-64]; no setter exists and
  optimizer_manager never assigns stage_ (it keeps its own search_stage_flag_
  [src/coordinator/optimizer_manager.cpp:990]). So stage_ is permanently Trunk and
  DIRECT_DILATION_T1 always takes the dilation-1 path; its "same dilation as comparison"
  branch [src/compute/DIRECT_DILATION_T1.cpp:42-57] is unreachable in the current wiring.
- Minor: DIRECT_DILATION_POLE_CONSTRAINT registers both "PoleWeight" and "Pole_Weight"
  (only PoleWeight is read); DIRECT_MAHFOUZ registers "Black_Silhouette" but never reads
  it; initializeDIRECT_DILATION calls AllocateCurvatureHausdorfScore for a metric that
  returns 0.

* Evidence on the Commented-Out DistanceMapMetric Term (question e)

The active cost already includes the distance-map term:
`metric_score += gpu_metrics_->DistanceMapMetric(...)` at
[src/compute/DIRECT_DILATION.cpp:79-82]. The commented-out block
[src/compute/DIRECT_DILATION.cpp:101-105] is a *second, identical* call to
DistanceMapMetric with identical arguments, placed after the biplane block. In monoplane
(the oracle config, and the only gated configuration today) uncommenting it would add the
same value a second time — a pure 2× reweighting of the chamfer term, no new information.
In biplane it would add a primary-camera-only chamfer on top of the A+B+A+B sum — an
asymmetric camera weighting, not a feature. Conclusion: dead code from experimentation,
not a deliberate design; the safe action is deletion (no behavior delta, R13-compliant).
What cannot be verified from the repo: its git provenance (no VCS access in this
environment) and whether the *active* distance-map term was added in the same change —
code-level evidence is conclusive regardless.

* What Could Not Be Verified

- D.R.E.C.T. paper full text (IEEE paywall; the refs/ PDF could not be text-extracted in
  this environment): the paper-side dilation handling and its per-axis accuracy numbers are
  inferred from the published abstract + repo comments (medium confidence). The repo-side
  description of the deviation is high confidence.
- The magnification arithmetic for the z claim (6.31 mm at ~1.1 m source distance → <1%
  scale → ≤~2 px on a ~300 px silhouette) is derived from baseline.json values
  (z_tran ≈ -1022.93, pitch 0.373) and an assumed source distance; the SID is not in the
  calibration file I read (medium confidence; the z-profile probe in Recommendations would
  measure the true profile directly).
- Per-variant ranking of z-sensitivity (chamfer-heavy > Mahfouz > pure overlap) is a
  hypothesis from metric semantics, not measured — no ablation harness exists yet.
- Whether SAME_Z/pole-constraint penalties at weight 1 are truly inert requires a DIRECT
  tie-breaking experiment; only the scale arithmetic is verified.
- Yamazaki 2004's specific depth-improvement technique (only abstract verified).
- The commented-out term's commit history and the origin of the (DIFFERENT FROM JTA PAPER)
  comment (no jj/git access in this environment).
- No public benchmark compares dilated-edge overlap vs chamfer vs gradient correlation on
  ML-segmented knee-implant fluoroscopy; all cross-family claims rest on adjacent-domain
  studies (spine, RSA, natural knee).

* Recommendations for JTML

Ordered by expected value per unit of risk. All are compatible with R2 (independent ground
truth), R13 (no behavior delta on production paths), and R15 (cumulative budgets
20k/25k/30k/35k stay load-bearing; only the metric term varies).

** 1. Build the metric ablation harness first — it is the missing decision tool

The seven registered variants have never been compared (no ablation harness exists;
test/unit/test_cost_function.cpp covers only the parameter registry). Concretely:

- Tier-0 (headless, CPU, CI): reference implementations of the metric semantics — overlap
  count + normalization, mean-chamfer, Mahfouz ratios — on synthetic binary images, in
  test/unit/. These pin the *intended* math (R13: tests only, no production edits) and
  would have caught bugs 1-4.
- Tier-1 (GPU, oracle label): run each variant on Kneel_1 (3 frames) under the production
  budget shape (cumulative 20k/25k/30k/35k, ranges from settings_constants.h) with the
  oracle's appearance gate (silhouette IoU > 0.85 vs Labels/fem/, independent of all cost
  math — this is the R2 guardrail: never validate a metric by its own optimum). Record
  recovered pose per axis, IoU, and per-axis gap vs fem.jts into test/golden/ablation.json.
  The oracle run is GPU-labeled; there is no `gpu` ctest label yet — reuse `-L oracle` and
  add the label as a follow-on.
- Z-profile probe (the single most informative new experiment): for each variant, sweep
  z_trans across the leaf range (±15 mm) around fem.jts with all other axes at truth and
  plot the cost curve. This measures the weak axis directly, is cheap (~31 renders/frame),
  and needs no production changes (the injected-cost boundary at DirectOptimizer /
  RunDirectStage already exists — src/coordinator/optimizer_manager.cpp:1225-1246).
- Headless path: the optimize_coordinator.cpp seam allows Tier-1 runs without the GUI;
  the z-profile probe is Tier-0/headless-able wherever the render can run.

** 2. Keep and re-weight the DistanceMapMetric (chamfer) term; delete the dead duplicate

It is the only z-sensitive term in the default cost. Recommended: (a) delete the
commented-out duplicate [DIRECT_DILATION.cpp:101-105] with a test asserting the cost value
is unchanged (R13); (b) calibrate the implicit blend weight of mean-chamfer vs the
pixel-count terms using the harness (the current blend is accidental); (c) document that
the chamfer runs on the post-dilation image and scores exact border pixels only.

** 3. Fix the four live bugs with tests first

bug-1 (uninitialized min_dist → UB) and bug-2 (Y_dist ≡ X_dist) in DD_NEW_POLE_CONSTRAINT;
bug-3 (tibia x translation replaced by z) in sym_trap_function; bug-4 (always-Trunk stage
guard makes DIRECT_DILATION_T1's non-trunk path unreachable). Each fix is a small,
behavior-visible change; pin the current behavior in deterministic unit tests *before*
editing (R13 relocation-style discipline), then fix, then re-run the oracle gate.

** 4. Extend the metric family in ROI order (all reuse existing infra)

- Symmetric overlap: normalize by union (IoU) or Dice instead of comparison-white-sum —
  iou.cu exists; removes the asymmetric bias that favors under-sized silhouettes (the z
  direction the current metric is least able to punish).
- Symmetric chamfer: render-side distance map on the projected edge so both sides are
  distance-transformed (true two-sided chamfer; classic Borgefors practice). DT infra and
  kernels exist.
- Curvature-weighted edges: weight the chamfer/overlap by local silhouette curvature using
  curvature_utilities.cpp (femoral notch, condylar and peg edges are the depth-informative
  ones); this connects to the stub CurvatureHeatmapMetric (currently returns 0) and is the
  most novel, lowest-cost z-informative option in the repo.
- Gradient correlation on DRR vs fluoro (or ML probability map): the literature-backed
  modern metric (Penney GC; Otake GO with masking caveats); requires wiring
  render_drr_engine.cu into the cost path — the largest item, justified only after 1-3 show
  the overlap family is the bottleneck.
- Learned similarity: leave to angle 03; the seam (segmentation output → cost) is already
  where the repo consumes ML.

** 5. Calibrate the geometric-prior weights (F family) against the pixel-count scale

SAME_Z and DIRECT_DILATION_POLE_CONSTRAINT penalties at weight 1 are ~0.01-0.3% of the
base cost. Use sym_trap's tuned scale (pole 75, vv 500) as the starting point and sweep
weights in the harness (e.g., Z_Weight ∈ {0, 10, 100, 1000}) on the z-profile probe. A
correctly weighted SAME_Z prior is the cheapest z improvement available, and it is
clinically grounded (tibiofemoral contact).

** 6. Ablate the documented deviation from the D.R.E.C.T. paper

After bug-4 is fixed, DIRECT_DILATION_T1 (rendered-side dilation 1 in trunk, same-dilation
otherwise) becomes a real ablation of the paper's asymmetric schedule vs the repo's
symmetric one — run it in the harness and record which schedule wins at each stage. This
turns an inherited, untested design decision into evidence.

** 7. Boundary honesty for the z axis

No metric in this landscape fixes monoplane depth by itself (Penney 1998, Yamazaki 2004).
The realistic targets: (i) shrink the z gap toward the ~1 mm / ~0.14 deg in-plane quality
already achieved (baseline.json notes) via 2+4+5; (ii) treat z > ~3 mm as a biplane or
multi-view problem (Subject0 biplane data exists but lacks Labels/ — an oracle extension
that the cost landscape alone cannot solve).

are the JTML action plan, sequenced so every step keeps the IoU gate and cumulative-budget
semantics intact.
