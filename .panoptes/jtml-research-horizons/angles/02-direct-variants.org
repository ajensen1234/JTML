* Summary
The repo's DIRECT (src/domain/direct_optimizer.cpp) is a faithful *JTA-flavored* Jones-1993: Jarvis-march lower-right convex hull over per-size-level best boxes, L2 side-diagonal size measure, single-largest-(denormalized)-side trisection, no epsilon parameter (effectively epsilon=0), no Lipschitz estimation, no local refinement. That is a legitimate, historically real configuration, but the 30 years of DIRECT research since 1993 overwhelmingly documents that (a) epsilon=0 invites the "excessive local refinement" pathology (wasting a huge fraction of an expensive budget over-refining a suboptimal local minimum — precisely the 20k trunk-budget risk here), (b) the highest-value, lowest-risk upgrades for a 6D / expensive-cost / 20k-35k regime are: an epsilon/refinement-control mechanism, the DIRECT-l infinity-norm size measure, ignore-ties (already effectively present), and a two-step global+local Pareto selection (DIRECT-GL), and (c) the trunk->2x branch->leaf stage design is a documented family pattern (multistage/zoom/recentering: HD-DIRECT, MrDIRECT, Jones-2001 revision, DIRECT-JTA itself) and is well-posed *provided* each stage's budget is not squandered by epsilon=0 over-refinement. No published variant is a magic bullet for 6D noisy expensive costs; the credible plan is a pure-seam A/B harness (analytic Tier-1 + oracle IoU Tier-2 as independent ground truth) with a default-variant golden baseline preserved.

* Key Findings
** F1. The authoritative landscape: Jones 1993 -> a rich, well-documented variant family
- The original algorithm: D. R. Jones, C. D. Perttunen, B. E. Stuckman, "Lipschitzian optimization without the Lipschitz constant," JOTA 79(1):157-181, 1993 (in repo refs/). Key properties: center sampling, trisection of ALL long sides ordered by best w_j first, potentially-optimal hyperrectangles = lower-right convex hull of (size, f(center)) points with epsilon parameter, convergence via everywhere-dense sampling. [Source: http://dumas.perso.math.cnrs.fr/DIRECT.pdf; http://www.planchet.net/EXT/ISFA/1226.nsf/769998e0a65ea348c1257052003eb94f/f9ca730ca27def69c12576d8002ed895/$FILE/Jones93.pdf]
- The authoritative survey by the original author: Jones & Martins, "The DIRECT algorithm: 25 years later," J. Global Optim. 79:521-566, 2021 (free preprint: https://websites.umich.edu/~mdolaboratory/pdf/Jones2020a.pdf). It categorizes every notable variant into five families: (1) selection modifications — DIRECT-l (Gablonsky & Kelley 2001), Finkel-Kelley additive-scaling fix, DIRECT-restart (adaptive epsilon), MrDIRECT/MrDIRECT-075 (Liu et al. 2015/2017), DIRECT-GL (Stripinis et al. 2018), ADC (Sergeyev-Kvasov 2006); (2) partitioning changes — DISIMPL (simplices), BIRECT (bisection, 2 points per box); (3) zoom-in — HD-DIRECT (Tavassoli et al. 2014); (4) local-search hybrids — Jones-2001 revision, DIRMIN, glcCluster, Gb-BIRMIN; (5) flexible partition + surrogate — eDIRECT (Voronoi + kriging). It concludes the generally-beneficial features are: hybridize with a local optimizer, split on one long side, select one of ties, exploit response-surface trends, keep an epsilon-like mechanism against tiny-rectangle waste, sample >1 point per region.
- Mature implementations: NLopt (C, LGPL) ships DIRECT, DIRECT-L, DIRECT-L_RAND (randomized tie-break), ORIG_DIRECT/ORIG_DIRECT_L (Gablonsky's Fortran v2.0.4), plus NOSCAL variants for anisotropic domains and a magic_eps parameter (https://nlopt.readthedocs.io/en/latest/NLopt_Algorithms/); SciPy scipy.optimize.direct defaults to the locally-biased DIRECT_L with max_fun capped at 1000*N and a 1GiB RAM cap (https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.optimize.direct.html); DIRECTGO is the GPL MATLAB toolbox implementing DIRECT v4.0 (Finkel), DIRECT-restart, DIRECT-m, DIRECT-l, DIRECT-G, DIRECT-GL, DIRECT-GLc, DIRECT-GLce, DISIMPL etc. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.02205, https://github.com/blockchain-group/DIRECTGO); pDIRECT-GLce for parallel (https://github.com/blockchain-group/pDIRECT-GLce); pygmo/pagmo has NO native DIRECT UDA — only via the scipy_optimize wrapper (https://esa.github.io/pygmo2/algorithms.html).
- Confidence: high. Relevance: this is the map for which variants are real, documented, and available as reference implementations to cross-check a C++ port.

** F2. What the variants change, and their documented effects (selection / splitting / Lipschitz / restart / dimension)
- DIRECT-l (Gablonsky & Kelley 2001): (a) selects only one of tied potentially-optimal boxes, (b) redefines size as half the longest side (infinity-norm measure instead of L2 center-vertex distance) => fewer size "levels", less global drag, faster refinement; documented downside: worse on hard multimodal problems (Sergeyev-Kvasov GKLS counterexamples). [Source: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10898-020-00952-6 (Sec. 3.2); https://nlopt.readthedocs.io/en/latest/NLopt_Algorithms/]
- Epsilon/refinement control: Jones-1993 epsilon in [1e-3, 1e-7] recommended; epsilon=0 documented as pathological (Shubert: >10,000 evals at 0.01% accuracy with epsilon=0 vs 2933 with 1e-4). Finkel & Kelley "DIRECT-restart" adapts epsilon (0 -> 0.01 after 5 no-improvement iterations, back to 0 on improvement, back to 0 after 50 stalls) — better on Shubert, worse on 48D Powell. DIRECT-m (median) and DIRECT-a (average) replace |fmin| by |fmin-fmedian|/|fmin-faverage| to kill additive-scaling sensitivity — but a 2021/2024 study found original DIRECT solved 10 and 23 MORE test problems than DIRECT-m/a on 81 test problems. [Sources: https://websites.umich.edu/~mdolaboratory/pdf/Jones2020a.pdf (Sec. 2.3.2, 3.1, 3.3); https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2109.14912 (Sec. 2.2)]
- Selection schemes beyond the convex hull: "aggressive" (select best box of EVERY size level; Baker et al. 2000 — good for parallelism, memory-hungry), "Pareto" (all nondominated boxes; PLO/PLOR, Mockus), "two-step Pareto" (DIRECT-GL: global step = nondominated on (f,size), local step = nondominated on (distance-to-current-best, size); no hyperparameters, no epsilon protection). DIRECT-GL converges faster on multimodal problems but is WORSE on unimodal ones and on Hartman 6 (8793 evals vs 571 for DIRECT) — a documented 6D caution. [Sources: https://websites.umich.edu/~mdolaboratory/pdf/Jones2020a.pdf (Sec. 3.4, 3.9); https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2109.14912 (Sec. 2.2)]
- Splitting: Jones-1993 trisects ALL long sides; the Jones-2001 revision and DIRECT-l trisect ONE long side (least-divided tie-break), which the survey shows drops evals for the 5-var linear problem from 14,492 -> 192. BIRECT bisects instead of trisecting with 2 diagonal sample points (aspect ratio <= 2:1, tighter Lipschitz bounds). [Sources: https://websites.umich.edu/~mdolaboratory/pdf/Jones2020a.pdf (Sec. 2.3.4, 3.8)]
- Lipschitz estimation: the DIRECT family eliminates the constant by testing all K>0; the Strongin-Sergeyev diagonal family instead ESTIMATES it with local tuning (weighted local+global estimates, reliability parameter r>1; sensitive to r). Not needed for DIRECT-type work but it is what the "no Lipschitz search" storage comment alludes to (the old JTA code had a Lipschitz-based mode). [Sources: https://websites.umich.edu/~mdolaboratory/pdf/Jones2020a.pdf (Sec. 2.1, 3.7); https://iris.unica.it/retrieve/473ab5ab-42de-485a-9804-4913db882347/AIP%20Kvasov%20Lera%20Sergeyev%20published.pdf]
- Restart/multistage: MrDIRECT/MrDIRECT-075 (multi-level DIRECT, W-cycle 21011012, epsilon per level) solved 72% vs 36% of GKLS problems (2-20 vars, budget 1000(n+1)) and 61% vs 11% at 20 vars; HD-DIRECT (zoom-in: rerun DIRECT in re-centered, shrunken bounds) cut a 15D sphere from >1,000,000 evals to 22,562. Both are the documented relatives of the repo's trunk/branch/leaf zoom structure. [Sources: https://websites.umich.edu/~mdolaboratory/pdf/Jones2020a.pdf (Sec. 3.6, 3.14)]
- Confidence: high (survey + primary papers). Relevance: every one of these mechanisms maps to a small, pure-domain change in DirectOptimizer.

** F3. Credibility for 6D / noisy / expensive costs with 20k-35k budget
- Dimensionality: Jones & Martins state DIRECT "usually performed well on low-dimensional problems (up to six variables)" — 6D is the top of the sweet spot (https://websites.umich.edu/~mdolaboratory/pdf/Jones2020a.pdf). Posik's BBOB-2009 benchmark found DIRECT "a viable alternative only for low-dimensional search spaces (5D at most)" — that is under BBOB's huge-budget/high-accuracy protocol, so it is a warning about high-accuracy 6D refinement, not about basin-finding at 20k-35k (https://sci2s.ugr.es/sites/default/files/files/TematicWebSites/EAMHCO/contributionsGECCO09/p2315-posik.pdf). The 2022 64-algorithm benchmark (800 GKLS + 397 DIRECTGOLib problems, >239,400 runs) found "deterministic algorithms perform excellently on GKLS-type and low-dimensional problems, while stochastic algorithms have shown to be more efficient in higher dimensions" — 6D is squarely the deterministic/DIRECT-friendly regime (https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.05759).
- Budget reality: on the DIRECTGOLib v1.2 set (287 problems), the fastest two-step-Pareto DIRECT variants solve ~33% (95/287) of problems within 1000 evals, ~78% (224/287) within 1e6 evals, at 0.01% relative accuracy; the curve's mid-section puts the repo's 20k-35k band somewhere around half the test set at that (very strict) accuracy (https://epublications.vu.lt/object/elaba:143490244/143490244.pdf, Table 2 + Fig. 4). For a coarse-accuracy pose problem (IoU gate, mm/deg targets) the effective success rate is far higher. MrDIRECT at budget 1000(n+1)=7000 for n=6 solves 72% of GKLS problems vs 36% for plain DIRECT (https://websites.umich.edu/~mdolaboratory/pdf/Jones2020a.pdf, Sec. 3.6). eDIRECT solves Hartman-6 in ~120 evals vs 571 for DIRECT (https://websites.umich.edu/~mdolaboratory/pdf/Jones2020a.pdf, Sec. 3.13).
- Expensive costs: the 2022 local-refinement study's headline for this regime: "when objective functions are expensive, local refinement reduction techniques improve performance, and 1-DTC-GL-gb is the best technique" — i.e., the epsilon/globally-biased controls pay off exactly when each evaluation is costly, which is the GPU-DRR case (https://epublications.vu.lt/object/elaba:143490244/143490244.pdf, Sec. 4). With cheap costs, plain 1-DTC-GL wins — meaning the repo's epsilon=0 is defensible only because its cost is cheap, which it is not.
- Noise: naive (deterministic) DIRECT on noisy functions "can perform well" but documented to lock onto false minima (Goldstein-Price with N(0,10): naive DIRECT stuck at actual value 3.17 vs true 3.0 after 3000 evals, DIRECT-S reached 2.917). Noisy variants (Noisy DIRECT / DIRECT-S) all rely on PLURAL sampling per point (Bayesian/OCBA resource allocation), which multiplies cost — incompatible with a 20k-35k GPU budget unless noise is small (https://mason.gmu.edu/~pnichol6/docs/direct-s_2015.pdf; https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~ferris/papers/siopt-direct.pdf). So: keep the deterministic core, buy noise-robustness through refinement control and through the Tier-2 oracle gate, not through replication.
- Confidence: high for the dimensionality/budget statements; medium for the exact success-rate interpolation at 20k-35k (the published curves are at 1e3..1e6 and 0.01% accuracy, not at the repo's budgets/accuracy).
- Relevance: directly answers "which variants are credible": DIRECT-l size measure, epsilon/δ-limit refinement control, one-per-level tie handling, two-step Pareto local step, zoom/recentering between stages. NOT credible at this budget: plural-sampling noisy DIRECT, eDIRECT-style kriging overhead (probably fine but bigger change), high-accuracy refinement claims.

** F4. Fidelity of the repo implementation to Jones-1993, and its known pathologies
- Verified by reading the code (src/domain/direct_optimizer.cpp, include/domain/direct_data_storage.h, src/domain/data_structures_6D.cpp):
  - Convex hull = Jarvis gift-wrap over per-size-level columns (minimum_value_columns_), starting from the largest size, walking left while slope >= 0, no epsilon condition anywhere => Jones-1993 hull with effectively epsilon=0. Ties: only the best box per size level is trisected (GetMinimumHyperbox + DeleteHyperBoxes of "the Best Hyperbox at the List of Column IDs"), i.e. one-per-level behavior closer to DIRECT-l than to the 1993 all-ties rule.
  - Size measure = L2 norm of the full side vector (data_structures_6D.cpp: "size_ = sqrt(sides.x^2 + ... + sides.za^2); /*L2 Norm*/") — exactly 2x Jones' center-vertex distance, equivalent up to a uniform factor (hull unchanged). This is NOT Gablonsky's infinity-norm measure.
  - Splitting = trisect ONE side, chosen as the largest DENORMALIZED side (TrisectPotentiallyOptimal -> DenormalizeRange -> GetLargestDirection). Jones-1993 trisects ALL long sides; single-side trisection is the Jones-2001 revision; choosing by largest denormalized (physical) side is a JTML-specific dimension-weighting adaptation with no direct literature counterpart (the equal-weight unit-cube assumption is explicit in most variants; NLopt's NOSCAL variants are the only documented relatives).
  - No epsilon parameter, no Lipschitz estimation, no restart, no local refinement, no Pareto step. The storage header says it is "different (faster) than one in JTA but doesnt support Lipschitz search" (include/domain/direct_data_storage.h:10).
  - Budget: seed center evaluation counted unconditionally (1 call even at budget 0 — asserted in test/unit/test_direct_optimizer.cpp); loop guard while(calls+offset < budget) with calls growing 2 per trisected box => the loop can overshoot the budget by up to ~2*(#selected in the final iteration) - 1 (asserted: GetCostFunctionCalls() >= 1000 for budget 1000).
- Documented pathologies that apply to this configuration: epsilon=0 over-refinement of suboptimal local minima (Shubert example, 25-years survey Sec. 2.3.2); boundary optima approached but never sampled (Sec. 2.3.5) — relevant to z-limits; slow high-accuracy refinement (the err<4.0 Tier-1 bar is a basin-quality bar, consistent with the literature); memory growth (DIRECT keeps every box; the storage keeps all boxes per level).
- The DIRECT lineage: Flood & Banks 2018 (IEEE TMI 37(1):326-335, doi:10.1109/TMI.2017.2773398 — in repo refs/) introduced the trunk/branch/leaf multi-stage DIRECT for exactly this application; the successor paper (Jensen et al., J. Arthroplasty 38(10):2068-2074, 2023, https://repository.uantwerpen.be/docstore/d:irua:17814) names it DIRECT-JTA and describes "three stages, the 'trunk,' 'branch,' and 'leaf.' Each stage was assigned distinct cost function parameters and search regions... Successively decreasing the dilation coefficient allowed the optimization routine to escape local minima, and the leaf branch served to find the optimal out-of-plane translation." So the repo's stage design (dilation 6/3/1, cumulative 20k->25k->30k->35k, z opened in leaf) is faithful to its own lineage; the stage-BUDGET numbers (20k/2x5k/5k) live in the repo PDF and could not be independently re-verified this run (paywalled; see What Could Not Be Verified).
- Confidence: high for the code-fidelity facts (read directly); high for the pathology mapping (survey + MDPI study); medium for DIRECT budget specifics.
- Relevance: R15 ("preserve validated DIRECT logic") is compatible with adding variants as new, clearly-scoped code paths; the current configuration's main *quantitative* risk is that 20k of the budget can be burned over-refining a suboptimal basin before the branch stage ever sees a fresh dilation — exactly the pathology the literature's refinement-control mechanisms target.

** F5. Principled evaluation: Tier-1 analytic + oracle IoU as independent ground truth
- The repo already has the right skeleton: test/unit/test_direct_optimizer.cpp (center-min exact, shifted-min err<4.0 in 6D, budget guard, seed accounting, determinism, offset semantics, callbacks) + hegel PBT (test/unit/test_direct_optimizer_properties.cpp) + the injected-cost seam (DirectOptimizer with std::function cost) that makes variant A/B testing 100% headless and CI-runnable.
- The DIRECT literature's standard battery to add: DIRECTGOLib v1.2 (287 problems incl. 6D cases like Hartman-6, Shekel, AckleyN; MIT-licensed, https://github.com/blockchain-group/DIRECTGOLib), the GKLS generator (controllable difficulty/known minima; used by MrDIRECT/ADC/2209.05759), and the Hedar test set (68 problems up to 48 vars; used by MrDIRECT-075 and DIRECT-GL) — with the standard success criterion of percent-error <= 0.01% and operational-characteristics reporting (proportion solved vs evaluation budget) (https://epublications.vu.lt/object/elaba:143490244/143490244.pdf; https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.05759). Noise-injection protocol exists too: Goldstein-Price with N(0, sigma^2), as in the DIRECT-S paper (https://mason.gmu.edu/~pnichol6/docs/direct-s_2015.pdf).
- The oracle appearance gate (IoU > 0.85, golden_oracle.org, Tier-2) is a legitimate, rare asset: most DIRECT benchmarks compare against known analytic f*; an independent appearance/pose ground truth satisfies R2 anti-circularity and is the right arbiter for GPU-tier behavior, complementing (not replacing) the Tier-1 analytic battery. The documented ~6.3mm z_trans weak axis (local-recon) is the natural measurement to track per variant.
- Confidence: high (protocols are directly documented in the cited studies; the seam exists in-repo). Relevance: the cost of a principled variant study here is low — pure domain, no GPU — and it directly de-risks R15 (golden baseline = current variant with all Tier-1 assertions locked).

* What Could Not Be Verified
- "DIRECT-optimistic" as a named variant: despite three targeted searches I could not locate a canonical publication titled/defining "DIRECT-optimistic". The documented nearest concepts are (a) DIRECT's own "optimistic" selection — "simultaneous searches using all possible constants from zero to infinity" (Jones-1993 abstract) versus pessimistic/valid Lipschitz estimates (Strongin-Sergeyev local tuning with reliability parameter r>1); and (b) the "improved original selection strategy" (ignore ties) in the empirical study https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2109.14912. If the team's "DIRECT-optimistic" refers to a specific paper, it needs a citation to be actionable. Confirmation path: search the DIRECTGOLib/DIRECTGO issue tracker and Stripinis-Paulavicius monograph (https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-031-46537-6).
- Exact stage budgets and dilation coefficients of the DIRECT paper (Flood & Banks 2018): IEEE paywalled; the repo's refs/ PDF is the primary source and should be treated as authoritative (local-recon notes the authoritative budget prose is 20k/2x5k/5k, and golden_oracle.org's 10k/stage prose is stale). I verified the stage STRUCTURE (trunk/branch/leaf, decreasing dilation, leaf = out-of-plane translation) via the successor paper (https://repository.uantwerpen.be/docstore/d:irua:17814) but not the numbers.
- Whether the repo's GPU cost noise is additive random noise (DIRECT-S/Noisy DIRECT regime) or deterministic discretization noise (kernel/rasterization): the noisy-DIRECT literature cited assumes stochastic replication and is only relevant if the GPU cost has run-to-run stochasticity (angle 05 territory). If the cost is deterministic per pose, the DIRECT literature's noise machinery is moot and refinement-control + oracle gating is the right response.
- Success rate at exactly 20k-35k evals for 6D analytic problems: published operational-characteristics curves are at 1e3-1e6 evals and 0.01% accuracy; the value for the repo's budgets/accuracy is an interpolation, not a published number. A 6D subset run on the headless seam would produce it directly.
- Quantitative benefit of DIRECT-l's infinity-norm measure specifically on 6D: reported on the original DIRECT test set (2-6 vars) and GKLS (2-5 vars) — "generally outperforms, sometimes marginally"; the 6D pose cost with the z-opened leaf range has no published analogue, so it must be measured in-repo.

* Recommendations for JTML
1. Add a variant switch to the pure seam, keep the Jones-1993 core untouched (R15). DirectOptimizer (src/domain/direct_optimizer.cpp) is the natural host: an Options struct (epsilon / delta-limit / size-measure / selection-mode) with defaults reproducing today's behavior exactly, so all Tier-1 golden tests in test/unit/test_direct_optimizer.cpp must pass bit-identically for the default variant. This is a pure-domain change — no GPU, no coordinator changes — and satisfies the "never silently re-derive" discipline by making the baseline explicit.
2. First experiment: refinement control (epsilon or delta-limit, or Finkel-Kelley DIRECT-restart's adaptive epsilon). This is the highest-evidence, lowest-risk fix: the literature documents epsilon=0's over-refinement pathology, and the 2022 study shows refinement reduction pays off specifically when costs are expensive (GPU DRR). The trunk's 20k evaluations are the biggest single exposure — a suboptimal-basin over-refinement there poisons every later stage.
3. Second experiment: DIRECT-l size measure (half longest side, infinity-norm) with one-per-level selection (already effectively present). Expect fewer levels/less global drag in 6D; validate on the Tier-1 set because the documented failure mode is hard-multimodal problems (GKLS evidence).
4. Third experiment (larger): two-step Pareto selection (DIRECT-GL/1-DTC-GL) — its local step (nondominated on distance-to-current-best) directly targets the documented weak z-axis by focusing refinement near the incumbent; but its documented Hartman-6 regression (8793 vs 571 evals) is a red flag for 6D, so it must earn its place on the analytic battery before any GPU spend.
5. Keep the trunk->2x branch->leaf structure with cumulative budgets — it is the documented multistage/zoom pattern (Jones-2001 revision, MrDIRECT, HD-DIRECT, and DIRECT-JTA itself). Two well-posedness notes from the literature: (a) re-center and optionally shrink each stage's bounds around the incumbent (HD-DIRECT zoom-in) so the leaf's widened z does not re-explore already-rejected territory; (b) the z-opened leaf range combined with the denormalized-largest-side split is an implicit dimension weighting — keep it, but document it as a deliberate deviation from the equal-weight unit-cube assumption, and test the L2-vs-L-infinity size measures under that anisotropy (NLopt's NOSCAL variants are the documented analogue).
6. Budget discipline: keep the documented overshoot semantics (loop exits after crossing the cumulative budget; final iteration can add ~2x#selected evals) but add a test asserting a bounded overshoot at 35k, and consider a pre-trisection budget check if hard GPU-cost enforcement matters.
7. Evaluation harness (cheap, headless): extend test/unit/test_direct_optimizer.cpp with a small analytic battery — 6D quadratic (exists), Goldstein-Price with/without N(0,sigma^2) noise, Hartman-6, and a rotated/shifted multimodal 6D case — and report operational characteristics (proportion of problems solved vs budget) at exactly 20k/25k/30k/35k, mirroring the DIRECTGOLib protocol (0.01% pe success criterion). DIRECTGOLib (MIT) and GKLS can be vendored into test/ as pure C++ with zero deps. Keep the Tier-2 oracle IoU>0.85 gate as the independent ground truth (R2) and track the ~6.3mm z_trans gap per variant.
8. Reference implementations for cross-checking: NLopt (LGPL C) and Finkel's DIRECT v4.0 / DIRECTGO (GPL MATLAB) are fine as *oracles in the test harness* (compare final points/values on the analytic battery) but should not be copied into the AGPL repo; the variants are each ~50-150 lines and are fully specified by the papers above.
9. Explicit non-goal: do not adopt plural-sampling noisy-DIRECT (DIRECT-S/Noisy DIRECT) at this budget — replication multiplies GPU cost and the documented gain applies to stochastic-simulation regimes, not deterministic GPU kernels; if cost noise is later found to be run-to-run stochastic, revisit (angle 05 evidence first).
10. Sequencing: the analytic harness + variant switch is a single pure-domain cut (headless CI, no oracle run needed); the GPU-tier verdict for any variant that survives Tier-1 should be one oracle-label run per variant on the existing 3-frame femur set, reusing golden_oracle.org's two-tier gate.
