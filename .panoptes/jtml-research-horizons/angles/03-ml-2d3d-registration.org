* Summary
The community's proven pattern for implant kinematics from fluoro is *not* end-to-end learned pose regression — it is segmentation-first (binary silhouette from a CNN) followed by classical Lipschitzian/DIRECT optimization refinement, exactly the architecture JTML already implements. The single most important finding: the repo's DIRECT-JTA refinement stage (trunk/branch/leaf, dilated-contour L1 cost, decreasing dilation) is verbatim the published, independently validated stage 3 of Jensen et al. "Joint Track Machine Learning" (J Arthroplasty 2023, same lab lineage: Flood, Banks, Rullkoetter), which reports external-validation RMS errors of 0.8 mm / 2.6 mm / 0.5-1.8° per DOF against RSA and motion capture. The ML pathway is best understood and invested in as an *initializer* (the paper's stages 1-2: CNN segmentation + fast initial pose), not as a replacement for DIRECT; single-plane out-of-plane translation is the universally documented weak axis for every method including learned ones. Training labels are generated synthetically from known poses via model rendering in every successful pipeline surveyed (no clinical annotation needed) — a pattern JTML's own renderer and Study2Grid generator already fit.

* Key Findings
** Finding 1 — The repo's DIRECT path IS the validated stage 3 of the same lab's published pipeline
- Jensen et al. 2023 "Joint Track Machine Learning" (arXiv:2205.00057, J Arthroplasty; UF Mechanical & Aerospace + Denver; authors Jensen, Palm-Vlasak, Flood, Burton, Rullkoetter, Banks) describes exactly the JTML architecture: (1) CNN segments femoral/tibial implants, (2) initial pose from comparing segmented contour to a shape library, (3) a modified Dividing Rectangles (DIRECT) routine called DIRECT-JTA with a three-stage "trunk/branch/leaf" search, an L1 cost between the *dilated contour* of the segmentation and the projected implant, and successively decreasing dilation coefficients to escape local minima. This matches the repo's optimizer_manager (trunk→branch→leaf, cumulative budgets 20k/2x5k/5k) and the DIRECT_DILATION default (FastImplantDilationMetric + DistanceMapMetric, dilation schedule 6/3/1) [Source: https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.00057]
- Reported accuracy: sub-mm RMS for in-plane translations vs human-supervised kinematics; independent RSA validation (113 image pairs): 0.8 mm AP, 0.5 mm SI, 2.6 mm ML, 1.0° FE, 1.2° AA, 1.7° IE; motion-capture validation (45 images): 0.72/0.31/1.82 mm and 0.56/0.63/0.84°. Evaluated on 8,248 images, 71 patients, 7 manufacturers, 36 distinct implants [Source: https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.00057]
- Implication for JTML: the DIRECT route is not merely "the validated primary path" internally — its refinement stage has peer-reviewed accuracy evidence against *independent* modalities (RSA, mocap), which is exactly the R2 anti-circularity standard the repo's oracle discipline wants. The classical path is the defensible one today.
- Confidence: high (peer-reviewed, independent validation, direct architectural match). Relevance: directly validates optimizer_manager + DIRECT_DILATION design; gives the repo external accuracy targets to cite.

** Finding 2 — Segmentation-first + optimization refinement is the dominant production architecture, not end-to-end regression
- The same three-stage shape (segmentation → fast init → robust optimization refinement) recurs independently: ETH's DAMN (Vogl et al., PLOS ONE 2022): "shadownet" (DeepLab-v3/ResNet-50) extracts binary implant shadows, "synthnet" (ResNeXt-50) regresses pose, and the paper explicitly recommends using the CNN pose as *starting pose for a pose-refinement optimisation* [Source: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0270596]; LUMC's integrated contour detection + pose estimation (Prins et al., Proc IMechE H 2011) [Source: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0954411911407669]; Flood & Banks 2018 (already in refs/) uses edge-detected/dilated matching + DIRECT with no segmentation step at all [Source: https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/8106803].
- Direct pose regression from single-plane has a hard ceiling: DAMN reports only 0.75 mm in-plane / **25 mm out-of-plane** / ~2° rotation at 50% of test samples (90%: 1.5 mm / 30 mm / 3-4°), degrading under occlusion and bilateral overlap [Source: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0270596]. In Joint Track ML, the NFD initial pose is "within a few millimeters or degrees" and the DIRECT stage brings it to sub-mm — regression alone is never the final answer [Source: https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.00057].
- Convergence: three independent groups (UF/Denver, ETH, LUMC) converge on segmentation/init + optimization. Confidence: high. Relevance: the deferred ML-integration plan should be framed as "better initialization for DIRECT," and the oracle gate should measure the *refined* pose, not raw estimator output.

** Finding 3 — Binary silhouette segmentation IS the right first step for metal implants — with one documented caveat
- All implant-focused pipelines use binary silhouette masks as the segmentation output: Joint Track ML feeds binary dilated contours to its cost [Source: https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.00057]; DAMN's shadownet is trained to output binarized shadows (Lovász-Hinge = smooth Jaccard loss, i.e. IoU-consistent) [Source: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0270596]; DeepFluoro (Grupp et al., IPCAI/IJCARS 2020) segments hip anatomy to drive registration [Source: https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1911.07042].
- The caveat is segmentation-error propagation: van der Kraats et al. (2004, IEEE TMI) showed interactive vs automatic segmentation contour variability maps directly to 3D pose variability [Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15598449/]. A competing school therefore *avoids* segmentation for pose: Mahfouz et al. "A robust method for registration of 3D knee implant models to 2D fluoroscopy" uses direct image-to-image similarity precisely because "the method does not require an accurate segmentation of the implant silhouette (which can be prone to errors)" [Source: https://orthoarchives.com/en/orthoscience/article/W2099752932]. JTML's dual-path design (DIRECT on raw/edge image for the classical route, silhouette path for ML) is exactly how the field hedges.
- The repo's `segment_image` threshold at `output > 0` (binary) with black-silhouette inversion handling matches the implant-community convention; the CUDACachingAllocator::emptyCache() per-frame pattern matches practitioner reports of low-VRAM (RTX 4070-class) sequential processing OOMs [Source: repo src/compute/machine_learning_tools.cpp + src/services/segmentation_controller.cpp].
- Confidence: high (multi-source convergence) on "binary silhouette is the standard first step for implants"; medium on "it is sufficient" (segmentation error propagation is documented). Relevance: keep the black-silhouette path; budget for segmentation QA (IoU vs renderer ground truth) as its own gate.

** Finding 4 — Synthetic labels from known poses via model rendering is the community-standard data play; no clinical annotation required
- Joint Track ML's segmentation labels were generated by rendering *flat-shaded ground-truth projections* from human-supervised kinematics — 6,284 train / 1,572 val images per implant network, 30 epochs, an A100 [Source: https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.00057]. DAMN pretrains the pose head on binarized rendered shadows, per implant combination [Source: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0270596]. DeepDRR (Unberath et al.) is the general-purpose synthetic-fluoro generator (physics-based DRR + noise + scatter) used across the field [Source: https://github.com/arcadelab/DeepDRR]. Metal segmentation models have been trained simulation-driven and released with weights (DeepDRR forward projection, FAU Erlangen) [Source: https://github.com/fuxinfan/Metal_Segmentation].
- Veriserum (ETH, arXiv:2509.05483): 110,990 dual-plane knee-implant images, 10 implant combos, poses from CAMS-Knee, robot ground truth ±0.05 mm, auto-registered poses MAE < 0.8 mm / 0.9° (differentiable PyTorch3D renderer + ADAM/NCC), 200 manual registrations as benchmark — explicitly offered for "implant segmentation, 2D/3D registration, pose initialization" [Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05483].
- Implication: JTML can generate its own training labels with the existing renderer (render_engine.cu silhouette at randomized poses around golden poses) — the same recipe the pipeline's authors used. The Study2Grid generator is a sibling of this idea. No external clinical data is needed to unlock the segmentation pathway.
- Confidence: high (four independent pipelines all use rendered-label training). Relevance: the "data availability" skepticism in the user's leanings is answerable — the bottleneck is engineering (label-generation harness + training), not data acquisition.

** Finding 5 — Learned models are implant-geometry-specific; per-implant personalization is the norm
- DAMN trains one synthnet per tibia/femur combination (53 combos; ~1 GPU-day each on a 2080 Ti after a 5-day shared pretrain; total ~63 GPU-days), because the same shadow implies different poses for different geometries [Source: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0270596]. Joint Track ML covered 36 distinct implant designs across 7 manufacturers [Source: https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.00057]. Veriserum's 10 combos (2 femur × 5 tibia) are likewise design-specific [Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05483].
- Implication for JTML: the env-var model contract (JTML_SEG_PT / JTML_FEM_ESTIMATE_PT, user-provided .pt per implant) matches community practice; but a segmentation .pt trained on the Kneel_1 femur should not be expected to segment a different implant design — the repo should record model metadata (implant design, training pose distribution, input size 1024×1024) alongside each .pt, and the oracle must pair models with their implant.
- Confidence: high (convergence across ETH and UF/Denver pipelines). Relevance: shapes the "commit model weights?" decision — weights are only meaningful bound to a specific STL + camera calibration.

** Finding 6 — Single-plane out-of-plane translation is the universal weak axis; learned methods don't fix it
- Independent documentation: Yamazaki et al. 2004 (IEEE) on improving depth position in 2D/3D knee registration — depth accuracy is "relatively poor because a change in depth causes only small changes in the 2-D silhouette" [Source: https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/1295080]; DAMN's 25 mm out-of-plane vs 0.75 mm in-plane [Source: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0270596]; Joint Track ML's largest RMS error is mediolateral (out-of-plane) translation at 2.6 mm [Source: https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.00057]; Veriserum motivates dual-plane specifically by "superior out-of-plane accuracy" [Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05483]; femur-insert collision work exists purely to constrain out-of-plane pose [Source: https://orthoarchives.com/en/orthoscience/article/W2071699836].
- The repo's documented ~6.3 mm z gap and ±15 mm leaf range on z (local-recon) are consistent with this fundamental limit, and the area-ratio z estimate (z = -pd·sqrt(sum_proj/sum_seg)) is a deterministic perspective-scale readout of the same weak signal the learned methods also under-perform on. Expecting ML to fix z from monoplane is unrealistic; DIRECT's leaf-stage z refinement (or biplane, below) is where z accuracy comes from.
- Confidence: high (five independent sources). Relevance: sets expectations for the ML pathway's oracle gate — IoU>0.85 silhouette agreement is dominated by in-plane + orientation accuracy; a pose-space z gate will show the gap.

** Finding 7 — The area-ratio-z + phaseCorrelate estimator: geometry is sound, validation is absent, and the learned-pose community has moved past its components
- The repo's estimator (src/services/implant_estimator.cpp) = orientation regression (3-float Z-X-Y Euler, degrees) → area-ratio z with clamp → cv::phaseCorrelate for x/y (shift × pixel_pitch·z/pd) → Euler recovery from R_y(θ_y)·R_x(θ_x)·R_g. No publication of this exact estimator was found in this research; it appears to be lab-internal (plausibly thesis material). Its two components have known properties: (a) phase correlation is a translation-only alignment method — it assumes the two inputs differ only by a shift; residual scale mismatch (z error) and shape mismatch (orientation error) degrade the peak and bias the shift, and Fourier-Mellin-style scale/rotation handling exists precisely because of this [Source: https://github.com/sthoduka/imreg_fmt; https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1007/978-3-642-37807-2_15]; (b) area-ratio z assumes projected area scales with 1/z² — valid for a fixed geometry and orientation, but orientation error changes projected area independently of z, and the clamp (ratio>1 → z=-pd) silently saturates.
- The learned-pose community standard is 6D rotation representations or se(3) Lie-algebra regression, because Euler-angle targets have discontinuities/gimbal-lock (DAMN uses 6D + geodesic loss; DiffPose regresses in the Lie algebra with geodesic losses) [Source: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0270596; https://arxiv.org/html/2312.06358]. The repo's Euler-recovery branch logic (gimbal-lock branches for A_32_ ≥ ±1) is a manual handling of exactly this known degeneracy.
- Confidence: medium (estimator provenance single-source; phaseCorrelate/area-ratio properties from the image-registration literature; Euler-vs-6D claim from two learned-pose papers). Relevance: if the ML pathway is invested in, a DAMN-style 6D rotation head with geodesic loss is the evidence-backed upgrade; the estimator's raw output should be validated on synthetic poses before being trusted as a DIRECT initializer.

** Finding 8 — Documented failure modes in the wild: symmetry ambiguity is #1, then occlusion and distribution mismatch
- Joint Track ML: 1 femoral blunder and 43 tibial blunders out of 392 test images; 66% of tibial blunders traced to symmetry ambiguity; an "ambiguous zone" (apparent varus/valgus < 3.6°) had an 11% tibial blunder rate vs 3.2% outside; blunder defined as squared-sum of rotation differences > 5° [Source: https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.00057]. The repo's tibial mirror disambiguation (ambiguous_pose_processing) targets the field's #1 documented failure mode — good.
- DAMN: occlusion (contralateral leg passing), bilateral implants overlapping, low contrast; the rendered-vs-real shadow distribution mismatch is called out explicitly; iterative re-rendering did NOT help (harder to train, more compute) [Source: https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0270596]. Joint Track ML's segmentation step crops the contralateral knee before feeding the CNN — a concrete pre-processing practice the repo's per-frame pipeline could adopt.
- Confidence: high. Relevance: the oracle's IoU>0.85 appearance gate would catch most such blunders (they are gross pose errors); the repo should log and categorize blunders per this definition for future model iteration.

** Finding 9 — Evaluation methodology: the field gates on pose-space errors with independent ground truth; IoU is a proxy, not the convention
- Community conventions: per-DOF RMS vs human-supervised / RSA / motion-capture ground truth (Joint Track ML) [Source: https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.00057]; in-plane L1 + geodesic rotation error (Veriserum, DAMN) [Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05483; https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0270596]; fiducial-based TRE with gold-standard phantoms (2D-3D-GS lumbar spine, Ljubljana; Visible Human standardized methodology) [Source: https://biprog.fe.uni-lj.si/en/raziskave/viri/2D-3D-GS/; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20964183/]; hip-joint 2D-3D gold-standard dataset with uncertainty modeling [Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9290855/]. The canonical survey situating all of these is Markelj et al. 2012 (MedIA) [Source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1361841510000368].
- The repo's IoU>0.85 appearance gate is a silhouette-space proxy (and a reasonable R2-safe proxy given the label set), but it cannot distinguish a 2 mm in-plane miss from a 15 mm z miss — the two have very different clinical meaning. The synthetic-label machinery (Study2Grid / renderer) makes pose-space gating nearly free: render at known poses, run the estimator, report per-DOF error.
- Confidence: high on conventions (5+ independent sources); the "IoU is a proxy" claim is an analytic statement grounded in those conventions. Relevance: add TRE/per-DOF gates to the ML oracle alongside the IoU gate; report in-plane vs out-of-plane separately (the field always splits these).

** Finding 10 — The modern learned-registration frontier (differentiable rendering) is real but needs CT volumes — a different product than JTML's STL-based pipeline
- DiffPose (CVPR 2024): patient-specific CNN pose regression pretrained on synthetic DRRs from the patient's own CT + test-time gradient refinement through a differentiable renderer (DiffDRR); sub-millimeter, evaluated on DeepFluoro (pelvis, 6 subjects, 366 images) and Ljubljana (cerebrovasculature) [Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2312.06358; https://vivekg.dev/DiffPose/]. xvr (MIT) claims patient-specific training in 5 minutes, ~100× faster than DiffPose [Source: https://xvr.csail.mit.edu/]. xreg (rg2, JHU) is the reference C++ library for intensity-based 2D/3D registration with segmentation-driven annotation (DeepFluoro labeling) [Source: https://github.com/rg2/xreg; https://github.com/rg2/DeepFluoroLabeling-IPCAI2020].
- These methods register X-ray to CT *volumes* of anatomy, not implant STL models; JTML's differentiable-rendering horizon would mean either (a) a differentiable version of render_engine.cu for gradient-based refinement over STL silhouettes (in the spirit of Veriserum's PyTorch3D auto-registration, which achieved MAE < 0.8 mm / 0.9° on implant projections [Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05483]) or (b) adding CT-volume inputs. Option (a) is a plausible long-term upgrade to the DIRECT refinement stage; option (b) changes the data contract.
- Confidence: high on the existence/claims of DiffPose/xvr (peer-reviewed + open-source code); medium on the specific fit to JTML (analytic). Relevance: confirms the user's instinct that learned registration has advanced — but the nearest-term, evidence-backed ML investment is segmentation + initialization, not differentiable rendering.

* What Could Not Be Verified
- No public validation numbers exist for the repo's exact estimator (orientation regression + area-ratio z + phaseCorrelate + Euler recovery). It may be documented in an unpublished thesis (e.g., P. Flood's or A. Jensen's) not surfaced by this search; the repo's own oracle skips without user .pt files, so no in-repo numbers are available either.
- Whether any published method can pass the repo's oracle gate as currently written (IoU > 0.85 on the *raw estimator output*, before DIRECT refinement). DAMN's 25 mm out-of-plane error at 50% suggests regression-only output would fail a strict silhouette gate; but no direct evidence either way.
- Data access terms for CAMS-Knee/Stan (requires request/ethics per ETH and OrthoLoad pages); whether Veriserum's Zimmer Biomet-derived implant geometries overlap with the repo's example implants.
- Whether the Euler-triple orientation head (3-float Z-X-Y output) is what the original lab models actually used — the community's learned-pose papers all use 6D/se(3) representations, but the repo's contract (3 floats, degrees) may mirror the original unpublished models.
- torch 2.4.1 C++ (torch::jit) deployment issues specific to this Qt/CUDA stack — no community reports found; the #undef-slots include-order issue is a known Qt/torch integration annoyance but no canonical fix was found in this search.
- Whether DeepDRR's released pretrained metal-segmentation weights transfer to knee implant silhouettes in this repo's fluoro geometry.

* Recommendations for JTML
1. **Unlock the segmentation pathway with renderer-generated synthetic labels (highest-value, lowest-risk ML investment).** Replicate the Joint Track ML label recipe with existing assets: render the implant STL (render_engine.cu) at randomized poses around the golden poses + background variation → binary masks; train a small U-Net in torch; export torchscript; run the existing oracle with JTML_SEG_PT. Scale evidence: ~6.3k images/network is the published bar, not 100k [https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.00057]. The oracle's Labels/ already provides the validation set; add a renderer-generated held-out pose set for R2-safety.
2. **Reposition the ML pathway as the initializer for DIRECT, and gate the refined pose.** The field's consistent result is regression-only is insufficient (25 mm z at 50% for the best published regressor [https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0270596]). Plan 001's deferred item ("run trained model over original image") should end at: ML pose → DIRECT refinement (existing trunk/branch/leaf) → IoU appearance gate on the *refined* pose. This mirrors the published, validated pipeline and gives the ML path a realistic bar instead of a nearly impossible raw-output gate.
3. **Add pose-space gates to the ML oracle, split in-plane vs out-of-plane.** Using Study2Grid/synthetic poses: per-DOF RMS + geodesic rotation error vs known poses (Veriserum/DAMN conventions [https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05483]); adopt the field's blunder definition (squared-sum of rotation differences > 5°) and log blunder rate, categorized by tibial ambiguous zone (varus/valgus < 3.6°) [https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.00057]. Keep IoU>0.85 as the appearance gate — but it cannot arbitrate z; report z separately.
4. **Validate or replace the area-ratio-z + phaseCorrelate estimator before trusting it as an initializer.** Cheapest first step: a synthetic-pose harness (no models needed beyond the current estimator) measuring per-DOF error vs known poses, including the clamp saturation case (ratio > 1) and off-axis cases (phaseCorrelate's translation-only assumption degrades with scale/shape mismatch [https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1007/978-3-642-37807-2_15]). If a new model is trained anyway, use a 6D rotation head + geodesic loss (DAMN/DiffPose practice) instead of the 3-float Euler contract with manual gimbal-lock branches.
5. **Formalize the per-implant model contract.** Document that .pt models are geometry-specific (DAMN: per-combination retraining [https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0270596]); pair each .pt with metadata (implant design/STL hash, training pose distribution, input size, calibration) so the oracle cannot silently pair a model with the wrong implant. Keep the clean-skip env-var pattern (JTML_SEG_PT / JTML_FEM_ESTIMATE_PT) — it is a good CI citizenship pattern.
6. **Adopt the community's segmentation pre-processing practices.** Crop the contralateral knee / restrict to the implant ROI before inference (Joint Track ML practice); keep per-frame CUDACachingAllocator::emptyCache() for low-VRAM; keep the 1024×1024 input convention already used by the oracle test (matches the lab's published segmentation training [https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.00057]).
7. **Keep the DIRECT route as the accuracy backbone; treat differentiable rendering as a horizon, not the next step.** The DIRECT-JTA stage has peer-reviewed external validation (0.8-2.6 mm, 0.5-1.7° vs RSA/mocap [https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.00057]) — that is the repo's strongest evidence asset. DiffPose/xvr-style learned registration requires CT volumes and targets anatomy [https://arxiv.org/html/2312.06358]; the nearest learned-refinement opportunity is a differentiable silhouette renderer à la Veriserum's PyTorch3D auto-registration (MAE < 0.8 mm / 0.9° on implants [https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05483]) — worth a feasibility spike only after the segmentation pathway is live.
8. **If the ML pathway is to beat the monoplane z gap, biplane is the evidence-backed direction.** Dual-plane is motivated specifically by out-of-plane accuracy (Veriserum [https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05483]; DAMN notes dual-plane is a direct training extension [https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0270596]). The repo already has Subject0 biplane data (missing Labels/) — adding camera-B labels is the cheapest step toward a learned or validated biplane z.
